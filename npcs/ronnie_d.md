## Backstory
Ronnie is the third-string member of Eddie Malone's crew, behind Danny Finch. He dropped out of Kenmore High (class of '55) and talks more than he drives. His '51 Comet Drifter is tired and so are his excuses for why it's slow. He lives with his grandmother on Seventh Street — the working-class side of town, south of the railroad tracks. Eddie tolerates Ronnie because he's loyal and fills out the crew, but Ronnie is the hanger-on who talks too much.

### Thrown Out of the Frost Top (Spring 1958)
Ronnie arrived at the Frost Top drunk with two friends, started bothering Bonnie Carmichael — leaning too close, unwanted touching, talking louder after being ignored. Then he reached for Bonnie's notebook. Mrs. Yamamoto emerged from behind the counter: "Put it back." Ronnie smirked. "Put. It. Back." Every woman in the restaurant watched him silently. Not scared. Evaluating. He tossed the notebook back carelessly. Mrs. Yamamoto: "Not today." Lindsay Smith spoke from her booth without looking up from a parts catalog: "Almost like women enjoy eating without dealing with idiots." The whole restaurant laughed. Ronnie pointed at Lindsay: "You race one car and suddenly you think you're somebody." Mrs. Yamamoto walked to the front door and held it open. "Out." Ronnie left fast.

A humiliation he doesn't talk about. Years later, racers still warned newcomers: "You can get thrown outta Mel's for fighting. But you get thrown outta the Frost Top for disrespect."
