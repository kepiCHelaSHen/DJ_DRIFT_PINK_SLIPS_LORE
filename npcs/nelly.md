## Backstory
Nelly is a full-time dreamer. His '49 Comet Drifter project has been "almost done" for two years. Every week he finds something else that needs fixing. He lives on Pine Street and works at the lumber yard with Marcus Johnson (#55) and Gilbert Torres (#96). His Drifter will be beautiful — someday. Kenmore High class of '52.

Nelson is the older half-brother of Buddy Crane — different fathers, same mother. He helps Buddy at the strip on weekends but doesn't race himself. Where Buddy is cocky and loud, Nelson possesses the supernatural ability to blend into walls. Tall. Quiet. Brown work jacket. Always looks slightly apologetic for existing.

Nelson reads manuals. He blends into backgrounds. He never brags, never drinks too much, never revs engines for attention, never talks over people. Which means Winterberry Acres barely notices him.

He started staying late at Mel's after closing. Not in a creepy way. Not obviously. Just there. Reading manuals. Drinking coffee slowly. Helping stack chairs without being asked. Then fixing loose stools. Carrying supply crates. Changing burned-out lights. Never announcing himself. Never asking for attention.

**Fall 1959 — The Potatoes.** One cold October night, Diane was carrying potato sacks behind the diner when one split open across the wet pavement. Potatoes everywhere. Nelson silently appeared beside her. No speech. No flirting. No performance. Just quietly kneeling in the rain helping gather potatoes. "You always sneak up on people like this?" Nelson shrugged: "Usually they're yelling too loud to notice."

When Diane asked if he was trying to impress her, Nelson thought about it seriously. Then: "No." "No?" "I'm trying not to annoy you." That might have been the most honest thing any man at Winterberry Acres had ever said.

**November 1959 — The Power Outage.** Bad rainstorm. Most of Main Street dark. Mel's running on emergency lanterns and stubbornness. After closing, Nelson walked up carrying two coffees. Handed her one without speaking. They stood listening to rain hit the diner roof. Nelson asked: "Why racing?" Diane smiled slightly: "Everybody asks that like there's supposed to be one answer." Long silence. Then Diane: "For a few seconds... everything shuts up." Nelson understood immediately. No explanation needed. "You race?" "No." "Then how'd you know?" He stared into the rain. Then quietly: "Woodwork's kinda the same sometimes."

That was the moment — someone falling in love with the person, not the racer.
