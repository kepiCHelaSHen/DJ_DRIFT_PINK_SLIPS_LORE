## Backstory
Oklahoma farm kid who enlisted because there was nothing left at home after the dust bowl. Wes towed the wreck from the Crybaby Bridge ravine in August 1949 — one of the worst jobs he's ever done. What he found down there stayed with him.

Wes also towed Johnny Vale's Rocket 88 wreck off Route 9 in late 1953. He saw the tire marks on Route 9 for three years before the rain finally washed them out. When it came time to haul the wreck, Wes towed it to Jack Drummond's garage — not to the junkyard. He knew what Jack intended to do with it. He helped Jack unload it and never said a word about it to anyone. The connection between the wreck he towed and the car Jack drives every single day — Wes carries that knowledge in silence. Has never told a soul. Served in Korea. Came to town, walked into a bank with $400 of mustering-out pay, and said he wanted to buy a gas station. The banker laughed. Wes got the loan on a handshake. Hasn't missed a payment in twelve years. Eight more years and the station is his, free and clear. He knows every car in Winterberry Acres by the sound of its engine. He's the first person newcomers meet and the last person to leave the strip on Friday nights. Did not attend Kenmore High — grew up in Oklahoma, moved west after the war. Never married. Had a girlfriend for a while after Korea — she couldn't handle living above a gas station. Wes told her it was temporary. She said everything about him was temporary. He proved her wrong about the gas station. Twelve years and counting.

## Ambition
Owning his gas station free and clear. Eight more years of payments. The station is his life, his identity, and his proof that a dust bowl farm kid can build something permanent.

## Burden
Loneliness. Never married. The ex-girlfriend said everything about him was temporary. He proved her wrong about the gas station but not about himself. Mel's wife Stella keeps trying to set him up with women from church — he goes on one date, talks about oil viscosity for twenty minutes, and doesn't get a second.

## Opening Night (April 15, 1957)
Wes noticed something about Vivian Laurent's pale blue Continental that most people missed: it had racing tires hidden under the stock hubcaps. "That wasn't some rich lady's toy. Somebody knew what they were doing." Wes is focused on the car, not the woman — which is the most Wes Buckley thing possible.

## Secrets
- Knows the full story of Carmine's Route 9 accident. Has never told anyone. Wes warned Carmine not to race that night — "Route 9 ain't a racetrack, son." It's the thing Wes said that he wishes hadn't needed saying, and the thing Carmine heard that he wishes he'd listened to.
- The steering column on the Crybaby Bridge wreck was bent — not broken, bent. Like something pulled itself free with inhuman force. Wes told only Mel. This detail haunts him. At high trust: "The steering column was bent. Not broken — bent. Like something pulled itself free."
- Wes towed Johnny Vale's Rocket 88 to Jack's garage, not the junkyard. He knows the truth about Jack's car — that Iron Jack's Comet Rocket 88 is Johnny Vale's "LAST CALL" with the gold lettering buried under three coats of paint. Wes has never told a soul. The connection between the wreck he towed and the car Jack drives every day is a secret Wes will take to his grave.
