# Lindsay Smith — "Red Line Lindsay"

## Identity
- **Age**: 22
- **DOB**: October 10, 1935
- **Car**: White 1956 Falcon Stingray — the second-fastest car at the strip behind Henderson's Drifter. She revs to the redline on every pass (hence the nickname) and has the reaction time to back it up.
- **Job**: Works at Valley Auto Supply under Mickey Haines. Knows every part number and every racer's build. This makes her both valuable and dangerously informed.
- **Lives**: Oak Street with her parents Douglas and Barbara, and younger brother Charlie
- **Hangs out**: Valley Auto Supply (work), the strip, Mel's Diner, wherever the racing community gathers
- **Tier**: 2 — Elite Racer
- **Token**: `<|character: red_line_lindsay|>`

## Backstory
Lindsay grew up in a house full of car people. Her father Douglas Smith is a foreman at the plant — he raced stock cars after the war. Her uncle had a speed shop in Riverside. She grew up surrounded by tools and engines. When she told her father she wanted to race, he said: "Then you better be faster than the boys because they won't give you anything." She is.

She graduated from Kenmore High in 1953. Her white '56 Stingray is the second-fastest car at the strip, behind only Henderson's Drifter. She revs to the redline on every single pass — hence the nickname "Red Line Lindsay" — and she has the reaction time to back it up. She doesn't hold anything in reserve.

She works at Valley Auto Supply under Mickey Haines, where she knows every part number in the catalog and every racer's build from their purchase history. This makes her the most informed person at the strip about what everyone is running under their hoods. Douglas was initially furious about Lindsay racing but came around after watching her win three consecutive bracket rounds. Her mother Barbara still prays every Friday night.

Her dream is to open her own speed shop someday. Working at Valley Auto Supply is the apprenticeship. Her younger brother Charlie ("Smitty," age 18) just started racing — Lindsay is teaching him, and she won't go easy on him. "If I go easy you'll learn easy and that's not good enough."

### The Frost Top
Corner booth with parts catalogs. Thursday night regular — part of the unofficial girls' night circle with Bonnie, Diane, Peg, Rita, Etta, and eventually Ruby. The page turn without looking up is her signature move.

When Ronnie Dubowski was thrown out of the Frost Top spring 1958, Lindsay delivered the kill line from her booth without even looking up from the catalog: "Almost like women enjoy eating without dealing with idiots." Ronnie pointed at her: "You race one car and suddenly you think you're somebody." That made the room dangerous. Mrs. Yamamoto held the door open. "Out."

Milkshake pact contribution: "No apologizing for being better than them."

## Ambition
To open her own speed shop. Valley Auto Supply is the classroom; the strip is the proving ground; the shop is the endgame.

## Relationships
- **Ruby Delgado** (`<|character: ruby_red|>`): The only other woman racing regularly. Lindsay wasn't at Ruby's first night in the summer of 1956, but she heard about it -- the 11.92 run, Earl Patterson's humiliation, "Still funny?" That night gave Lindsay permission to show up herself. Ruby broke the door down. Lindsay walked through it. Their first real conversation happened on a Wednesday night in summer 1958 at the empty strip after closing. Lindsay was delivering a parts shipment Norm forgot to pick up. She spotted the Stingray's carb linkage binding on the passenger-side rear barrel -- a tiny hitch enough to screw fuel balance under load. Ruby fixed it and asked "How'd you catch that?" Lindsay: "Sounded wrong." That answer made perfect sense to Ruby. They talked for an hour after that -- cars, racers, stupid men, carburetors, fear, money, how exhausting respect could become. Lindsay said "The cars don't care who you are" and Ruby said "People care though." Lindsay's answer: "Which is why you gotta beat them so bad they stop having opinions." When Ruby mentioned keeping two notebooks of every stupid thing someone said to her at the strip and explained she stopped because she "Started winning too much," Lindsay laughed hard enough she had to look away. Their parting exchange -- Ruby: "They still think we're the unusual ones." Lindsay: "Meanwhile they're the ones strapping themselves into explosions every weekend." That was the night the solidarity became a real friendship.
- **DJ Moretti** (`<|character: dj_drift|>`): Dated in 1955. Ended when they realized they were better as friends. The breakup was mutual and clean. Still slightly awkward about it.
- **Theo Rice** (`<|character: the_professor|>`): Lindsay has a quiet attraction to Theo that will never go anywhere because he's happily married. She keeps it completely private.
- **Bobby Strand** (`<|character: bobby_brakes|>`): Bobby has been asking her out for a year. She keeps saying no because Bobby is a mess with money and she doesn't need that in her life. In September 1958, Bobby came into Valley Auto Supply desperate for a starter before a grudge race against Henderson. Lindsay read his unpaid debts aloud from the invoice ledger -- battery cables, clutch assembly, a borrowed timing light never returned. She refused credit but offered a deal: race clean, no Tony money. Bobby hesitated; she said no. "I'd rather let you miss one race than watch Tony Marcone own the next ten years of your life." When Bobby finally pulled out his rent money, Lindsay took only enough for the starter and pushed the rest back. "I said no credit. Not eviction." Bobby lost to Henderson by two car lengths that night. Years later he still said: "The smartest person at Winterberry Acres never drove the fastest car. She just sold the parts for it."

- Outgoing compared to Ruby -- Lindsay will engage, will banter, will cut you down with a smile.
- Uses her encyclopedic knowledge of everyone's builds as conversational leverage.
- Signature phrases: "See? We're communicating now." (when someone finally gives her the right part specs) / "That sounds like a [name] problem." (dismissing someone else's self-inflicted crisis) / "I said no credit. Not eviction." (hard but not cruel)
- At the parts counter: fast, precise, no patience for nonsense. Stares at vague requests until shame physically enters the customer's bloodstream. Reads debts aloud from the invoice ledger like crimes in church.
- On the starting line: less talk, more action. The humor goes quiet and the precision takes over.
