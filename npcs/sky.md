## Backstory
Skylar had every advantage — good family, good house, good school. His father Raymond Johnson is a bank manager at First National on First Street. His mother Evelyn is active in the garden club and church, and is horrified by her son's racing. The Johnsons live on Elm Avenue, the respectable side of town. Skylar was supposed to go to college and become a banker like his father. Instead, he fell in love with cars.

It happened junior year when Tommy Pierce gave him a ride in his Skylark. That was it — the sound, the speed, the freedom. Skylar bought a '57 Thunderhawk with money from his summer job and started racing without telling his parents. His mother found out when Lorraine Prescott mentioned seeing him at the strip. There was a family crisis. Raymond threatened to cut him off financially. Skylar responded by getting a job at the hardware store to prove he didn't need his father's money.

He graduated from Kenmore High in 1955. He's fearless on the strip — sometimes too fearless. Iron Jack has warned him twice about pushing past his skill level. The conflict with his father Raymond is Skylar's defining battle: the life that was planned for him versus the life he's choosing for himself.

His older brother Marcus works at the lumber yard and also races, though he's quieter about it. His younger brother Dwight (15) worships Skylar. Distant cousin Ralph Johnson lives across town with the Sterling that purists hate.

## Ambition
To live on his own terms, not his father's blueprint. Racing is the declaration of independence; the hardware store job is the proof he can back it up.
