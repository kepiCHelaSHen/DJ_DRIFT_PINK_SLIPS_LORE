## Backstory
Moved from Pennsylvania in '57. Wife Clara, no kids. Bought a '56 Falcon Bel-Star with cash. Wes Buckley was the first person he met in town. Quiet, observant. Learning the scene. Former machinist from Pennsylvania -- precision work, not factory line. His hands give it away: scarred knuckles, tiny burn marks, clean fingernails despite shop wear. Precision-worker hands, not racer hands. Did not attend Kenmore High.

In October 1958, Walt walked into Valley Auto Supply for the first time and asked for Hastings forty-thousand-over moly rings -- exact manufacturer, exact oversize, exact material. Nobody asks for that unless they know engines at a level most racers at Winterberry Acres don't. He claimed the build was for a friend and "mostly street." Both lies. Lindsay Smith caught them immediately from the parts request alone. Nobody buys moly rings for "mostly street" unless they plan on turning the engine into violence later. When Lindsay asked if he was balancing the rotating assembly himself, Walt looked up sharply -- recognition. Not attraction yet. Not trust. Recognition. Like realizing somebody else speaks the same strange language you do. His line to Lindsay: "You noticed the ring material before you noticed me." That landed harder than he intended, because he was right -- and because most men noticed Lindsay as a woman, a racer, a challenge, a problem. Walt noticed how her brain worked. That was different.

## Burden
Whatever happened in Pennsylvania. Walt hides competence like it's dangerous -- not from lack of confidence, but because somewhere before Winterberry Acres, being underestimated had kept him alive. He entered rooms hoping nobody noticed him. He waited patiently while other customers argued. He answered questions with careful pauses and practiced half-lies. Whatever the machining work was in Pennsylvania, it was more than factory work, and leaving it behind cost him something he hasn't explained to anyone.
