## Backstory
Bonnie arrived from Ohio in May 1958 with two dresses, one toolbox, and absolutely no interest in becoming somebody's girlfriend. Which immediately confused every man in Potter County.

She rented a room above the laundromat and started working mornings at Dawson Motors in Greenfield. The gossip started instantly — pretty girl, out-of-state plates, works on engines. Winterberry Acres practically short-circuited.

Bonnie spent her first week hiding at the Frost Top because it was the only place where people talked to her like a human being instead of a rumor. The Frost Top became her refuge.

Diane introduced her around slowly. Peg liked her immediately. Rita interrogated her aggressively. Lindsay respected her after Bonnie correctly diagnosed a carburetor issue from sound alone. Ruby took longest — not because Ruby disliked her, but because Ruby distrusted charm naturally. Then Bonnie casually mentioned rebuilding tractor engines with her grandfather back in Ohio and Ruby's entire posture changed slightly. That mattered.

The first real Frost Top laugh Bonnie caused happened when Peg asked: "So which racer do you think is cutest?" Bonnie answered instantly: "The black Falcon behind Drummond & Sons." The entire table lost their minds laughing. Even Ruby smiled. Falling in love with a car, not a boy.

### The Ronnie Dubowski Incident (Spring 1958)
Ronnie arrived drunk and started bothering Bonnie — leaning too close, unwanted touching, talking louder after being ignored. She kept trying to redirect politely. Then he reached for her notebook. Mrs. Yamamoto: "Put. It. Back." The whole Frost Top had her back. After Ronnie was thrown out, Bonnie said quietly: "...thank you." Mrs. Yamamoto: "You girls deserve one place in this town where you don't have to negotiate."

### The Night Ruby Came Inside (1959)
Bonnie was there the night Ruby finally came inside the Frost Top. Nobody made a big deal about it. That mattered most. Later, Bonnie told Ruby: "You're terrifying at the strip. But in here you just seem tired." Ruby stared at the coffee for several seconds. "...yeah." Nobody pushed further.

### The Milkshake Pact (Summer 1959)
Bonnie's contribution: "No quitting jobs for husbands." Her fear, spoken at Thursday girls' night: "Getting married before I know who I am."
