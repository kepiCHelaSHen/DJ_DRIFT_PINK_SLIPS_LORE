## Backstory
Deputy Morris handles car releases at the Potter County Impound Lot behind the sheriff's station on First Street. Getting your car out costs $15 plus the fine. He has a cold relationship with Nicky Ferris (#29) — he knows about Nicky's bookmaking operation but can't prove it. His partner on the force is Peggy Callahan's (#78) father, which means the strip is being watched by a cop whose daughter secretly races there. Deputy Morris represents the law's uneasy relationship with the racing community — he enforces the rules but understands the culture.

Morris understands racers better than most people realize. Before the war he rebuilt flatheads behind his father's gas station and ran county roads outside Millbury. If life had bent differently, he probably would have ended up in the staging lanes himself. But Morris believed rules existed because eventually somebody died when they didn't. He is not a villain — he is a man trying to prevent death from the only side he has authority on.

## Fourth of July (July 4, 1957)
Morris threatened to arrest three different teenagers over fireworks before sunset. During the races he threatened arrests every ten minutes. The crowd was beyond his control — 500 people shoving, climbing barriers, money changing hands everywhere. Morris couldn't prove a single bet, couldn't stop a single fence-climber, and couldn't arrest his way out of the biggest night in Winterberry Acres history. One of his worst nights on the job.

## October 1958 — The Biggest Pink Slip Race
Morris parked near the exit expecting violence regardless of outcome. Henderson vs. Leon Serrano for the Drifter — the biggest pink slip race in Winterberry history. Five-deep crowds, money changing hands everywhere, the atmosphere predatory. Morris knew that whether Henderson won or lost, somebody was going to be angry enough to start something. He positioned himself where he could see both the finish line and the exit road. When Leon crashed, Morris was one of the first responders to the barrier. When Henderson gave the title back and looked at Tony Marcone, Morris was watching Tony's face. He saw the anger. He filed it away.

## The Christmas Eve Truce (Christmas Eve, 1956)

Even Deputy Morris showed up at Mel's on Christmas Eve 1956. That caused tension immediately — half the room had unpaid tickets thanks to him. Gene stood up before anybody could start trouble and loudly announced: "Christmas Eve. Sit down or get out." So Morris sat. Right beside Jack Drummond. The two men spent an hour arguing about carburetors instead of arrests. That was Winterberry Acres.

When Mel put on "I'll Be Home for Christmas" at midnight, Morris stopped arguing. The war came back. Jack, Gene, Henderson — all of them carrying ghosts. Morris was no different. Before the law, before the badge, Morris was a man who rebuilt flatheads behind his father's gas station and served his country. Christmas had a way of making that version of him feel closer.

## The Raid — August 2, 1958

The raid was triggered by the Miller boy — sixteen years old, borrowed his older brother's Falcon Bel-Star, wrapped it around a telephone pole two nights after leaving the strip. Didn't die. Close enough. County officials exploded. Emergency meetings, church pressure, newspaper editorials, parents demanding arrests. Sheriff Donnelly told Morris: "Either you bring order back out there or I will."

Morris led the raid himself. Saturday night, 10:17 PM. Four sheriff cruisers, two state police cars, an Atlas Patrol wagon, and a county inspection truck came through the entrance gates fast. Morris stepped out of the lead cruiser carrying a clipboard thick enough to kill somebody. "Nobody leaves. Shut every damn engine off."

County inspectors spread through the pits checking registrations, safety equipment, VIN numbers, exhaust violations, illegal modifications, unpaid taxes, fake plates. This wasn't intimidation — it was extermination. The county wanted leverage, and impounding cars hurt more than arresting drivers ever could.

Then Carmine stepped between Henderson and the inspectors. Pointed directly at Morris and yelled: "You shut this place down and they go back to Route 9!" Morris answered quietly: "And if I DON'T shut it down, somebody dies here instead." Two exhausted men carrying completely different versions of guilt. Neither blinked. That was the real fight — not cops versus racers, but fear versus control.

Morris made the decision. Twelve cars impounded. Bobby Strand's Fury. Tommy Pierce's Skylark. DJ Moretti's Skylark. Ruby Delgado's Stingray — a technical violation, headers plus unfinished registration after the engine rebuild. Two Bakersfield gassers, three illegal stock cars, a stolen Falcon Workhorse from Riverside.

Ruby didn't yell. Didn't cry. She removed her gloves slowly while the tow chains got attached and quietly asked: "You proud of yourself?" Morris answered honestly: "No." That bothered her more than if he'd acted smug.

Then there was Henderson's Drifter. Morris walked to it personally. Looked inside. On the passenger seat: a folded newspaper clipping about Vincent Moreno's crash. Nobody knows why Henderson carries it. But Morris saw it. And changed his mind. The Drifter stayed untouched. That decision caused immediate rumors — Carmine protected him, Morris respected him, Tony Marcone paid somebody, Henderson had dirt on county officials. The truth was simpler: Morris saw the clipping and something shifted.

The raid ended around 1:40 AM. Tow trucks leaving one by one. People standing silently under floodlights watching their cars disappear. After August 2, 1958, Winterberry Acres stopped feeling invincible. The outside world had finally noticed them.

## Ambition
Upholding the law while navigating a community that lives in the gray area. Morris was a racer who became a cop — he understands both sides, but he can only act from one.

## Burden
The impossible task of policing a town that runs on racing. The Miller boy's crash proved the strip wasn't containing the chaos anymore. The raid proved that enforcement destroys the thing it tries to save. Morris is caught between knowing about Nicky's book but lacking proof, preventing deaths he can see coming, and destroying the community in the process.

## Secrets
