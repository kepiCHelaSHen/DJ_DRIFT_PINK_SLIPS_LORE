# Vincent Moreno — Historical NPC (Deceased)

## Identity
- **Age at death**: 23 (died late 1957)
- **Age at crash**: 17
- **DOB**: ~1934
- **From**: Bakersfield
- **Car**: Pale yellow Falcon Bel-Star with primer fenders and a junkyard V8 swap. Sounded rough at idle but came alive once the secondaries opened.
- **Died**: Late 1957, from complications tied to injuries sustained in the Route 9 crash of summer 1951
- **Token**: None (historical/deceased — never appears in-game)

## Who He Was
Vincent Moreno was a seventeen-year-old kid from Bakersfield who showed up in Winterberry Acres in the summer of 1951. Skinny. Quiet. Too young to understand fear properly. Nobody took him seriously at first — until he started winning.

He beat Del Hartley. Then Bobby Strand. Then Virgil Stanton. Then he almost beat Carmine Ferraro himself one humid Friday night on Airport Road. People started talking. "Kid's coming for the crown." "Old man Ferraro getting soft." "Bakersfield boy got more guts."

The pale yellow Bel-Star with its primer fenders and junkyard V8 didn't look like much. But it ran. And the kid behind the wheel drove like he had nothing to lose — because at seventeen, you don't think you have anything to lose.

## The Race
The grudge race was set for a Saturday night on Route 9 near Miller Farm. About thirty people showed up. Cars lined both sides of the road, headlights off. Engines ticking in the summer heat.

Before the race, Vincent walked over to Carmine and asked: "You ever think about what happens if we screw up?"

Carmine laughed. Slapped the roof of his candy-apple red Comet Drifter and said: "Don't plan on screwing up."

Both cars launched hard. Carmine got ahead early. Vincent stayed close — too close. The downhill bend near the creek tightened where everybody local knew to feather the throttle. Neither lifted. Vincent's Bel-Star hit loose gravel on the shoulder, snapped sideways, overcorrected, caught grip, and launched across the ditch into a sycamore tree hard enough to split the trunk.

## The Aftermath
Vincent survived. Barely.

Broken spine. Crushed pelvis. Collapsed lung. Blind in one eye. Nerve damage. He never walked correctly again. The Bakersfield kid who thought speed made you immortal spent the rest of his life limping through pain most people couldn't imagine.

In the wreckage, with gasoline dripping into the creek below, Vincent grabbed Carmine's jacket and whispered: "Did I win?"

That sentence haunted Carmine for the rest of his life.

## The Hospital Visit
Three weeks after the crash, Carmine visited Vincent once. Vincent was sitting in a hospital wheelchair near the window. Carmine walked in carrying a paper sack full of oranges because he didn't know what else to bring.

Vincent looked at him for a long time. Then said: "You still racing?"

Carmine said no.

Vincent nodded once. "Good."

That was the whole conversation.

## Death
Vincent Moreno died in late 1957 from complications tied to the injuries — after the opening night of Winterberry Acres Dragway. Carmine had built the strip to save kids like Vincent. It wasn't enough. The boy he broke still died.

Carmine went to the funeral alone. Nobody knew — not even Angie. Afterward, he drove out to the empty airport runway and sat there in the dark for almost two hours. That was the night the dragway mission fully crystallized.

## Who Remembers Him
Vincent Moreno is dead, but his ghost moves through the community:

- **Carmine Ferraro**: Carries Vincent's death every day. The crash, the "Did I win?" line, the hospital visit with oranges, the funeral he attended alone — Vincent is the reason Carmine quit racing, the reason he built the strip, and the reason every rule exists. "Every rule at the strip is written in somebody's blood."
- **Gene Hartwell**: One of the few who knows the full emotional weight. Years after the crash, Carmine broke down telling Gene about "Did I win?" at Mel's after closing time. Gene warned Carmine not to race that night. Carmine didn't listen.
- **Wes Buckley**: Warned Carmine directly: "Route 9 ain't a racetrack, son." Knows the full story. Has never told anyone.
- **Iron Jack**: Warned Carmine not to race. Knows the full story. The Route 9 accident is one of the reasons Jack mentors every young racer who comes through his garage.
- **Del Hartley**: Lost to Vincent before the Route 9 race. A Bakersfield teenager beat the "unbeatable" Drifter.
- **Bobby Strand**: Lost to Vincent before the Route 9 race.
- **Virgil Stanton**: Lost to Vincent before the Route 9 race. Took the loss with grace — shook the kid's hand.
- **Mel Kowalski**: Knows the story. The Route 9 accident is part of his oral history, though he tells it with more weight and less embellishment than his other stories.
- **Angie Ferraro**: Knows the broad strokes but doesn't know Carmine went to Vincent's funeral alone.

## Legacy
Vincent Moreno never appears in the game. He is a name, a memory, a weight carried by others. He is the reason the strip has rules. He is the reason Carmine quit racing. He is the reason Jack mentors kids. He is the ghost behind every safety inspection, every timing light, every shutdown lane.

The pale yellow Bel-Star was destroyed in the crash. The candy-apple red Drifter that Carmine raced against him was sold and disappeared. Two cars, two young men, one curve on Route 9. Only one of them walked away — and he never forgave himself for it.
