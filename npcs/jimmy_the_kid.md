# Jimmy Dawes — "Jimmy the Kid"

- **DOB**: April 20, 1939
- **Car**: 1953 Falcon Bel-Star — given to him by Iron Jack as a graduation present. Jack had taken it in trade from a customer who couldn't pay. Jimmy built the motor himself under Jack's supervision. First pass: 13.1. Most recent: 12.4. Improving faster than anyone has ever seen.

- **Lives**: Seventh Street (not far from the Malones) with his mother Elaine and younger brother Robert (14)
- **Hangs out**: Drummond & Sons Garage (spends more time there than at home), the strip, Mel's Diner, DJ's Drive-In
- **Tier**: 2 — Elite Racer

## Backstory
Jimmy's father Howard Dawes died in Korea in 1951. Jimmy was twelve. His mother Elaine works at the Piggly Wiggly — same shift as Ruby's mother Carmen, which is how Jimmy first met Ruby at a grocery store picnic. He has a younger brother Robert (14) who follows him everywhere. The Dawes family lives on Seventh Street, working-class side of town.

Jimmy grew up without a father and found one in Iron Jack. He started hanging around Drummond & Sons Garage at 14, sweeping floors, organizing tools. Jack saw something in him — patience, good hands, a quiet focus that the loud kids at the strip would never have. By 16, Jimmy was doing valve adjustments. By 17, he was rebuilding carburetors. Jack gave him the '53 Falcon Bel-Star as a graduation present — a car Jack had taken in trade from a customer who couldn't pay.

Jimmy built the motor himself under Jack's supervision. His first pass at the strip was a 13.1. His most recent was a 12.4. He's improving faster than anyone has ever seen. Gene Hartwell says Jimmy reminds him of a young Jack Drummond — the highest praise anyone at the strip has ever given.

He just graduated from Kenmore High in June 1957. He's too focused on racing and the garage to date seriously, though he has a crush on Sofia Moretti (DJ's sister, works at the drive-in ticket booth). She's aware of it and thinks it's cute but hasn't encouraged it.

## Jack's Last Race (Fall 1957)

Jimmy watched his mentor race the Deuce. He was there when Jack rolled the old black '34 into staging — no trailer, no crew, no showmanship. Jimmy had seen the Deuce in the garage a hundred times, had helped Jack keep it running, but he'd never seen Jack drive it in anger. The launch was everything Jack had ever taught him: controlled, patient, efficient. No wasted motion. No ego. Just a man who knew his car and knew the strip and didn't need to prove anything to anyone.

Jack won by two car lengths. The crowd roared. And Jimmy understood — in a way he couldn't have explained to anyone — what Jack had been teaching him all along. Not how to be fast. How to be right. Control and patience beat raw speed every time. That was the lesson of the Deuce. That was the lesson of Iron Jack. Jimmy had heard it in the garage a thousand times, but that Saturday night he finally saw it.

This is a formative moment for Jimmy. The night the teaching stopped being words and became proof.
