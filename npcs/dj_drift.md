## Backstory
DJ got his nickname because he started as a record spinner at high school dances at Kenmore High (class of '50). "DJ" just stuck — nobody calls him Dominic. His father Dominic Moretti Sr. built DJ's Drive-In in 1948 and ran it until a heart attack in 1955. DJ took over at 23, turning the drive-in into the center of youth culture in Winterberry Acres. His mother Lucia still makes the popcorn on weekends.

He has two younger sisters: Gina (22, married, lives in Riverside) and Sofia (18, works the ticket booth at the drive-in — and is the object of Jimmy Dawes's crush). The Morettis are a close Italian family rooted in Vine Street.

By day, DJ runs the business — scheduling movies, ordering supplies, managing the carhops. By night, he races the '55 Skylark that he and Tommy Pierce built together in Drummond's garage. He's fast enough to be elite but doesn't take it as seriously as the top guys because the drive-in is his real passion. He wants to open a chain of drive-ins across the country. The racing is the hobby. The business is the dream.

DJ is the social hub of Winterberry Acres. The drive-in is where all the car guys gather, and DJ is the host, the MC, the guy who knows everyone's name and everyone's story. He's the unofficial announcer at the strip — Carmine trusts him with the PA system and event coordination.

## Ambition

## The Christmas Eve Truce (Christmas Eve, 1956)

DJ decorated the jukebox with tinsel and somehow avoided electrocution. When Henderson walked in and stood frozen in the doorway, DJ yelled: "Hey Henderson, you miserable bastard, sit down before Christmas ends." Somehow that worked.

Later DJ convinced everybody to sing Christmas songs. Terrible mistake. Half the racers couldn't sing. The other half were drunk enough to think they could. Ruby refused completely. Henderson mouthed maybe four words total. Jack sang loud enough for everybody. Tony Marcone knew every lyric — that disturbed people more than if he'd carried a gun.

## Ruby's First Night (Summer 1956)
DJ took fake bets on Ruby Delgado's first night at the runway races. Started joking with the crowd — comedy money on a girl who thought she could race. Then Ruby ran 11.92 and DJ stopped joking. He tells this story now as a lesson in humility: "I was taking comedy bets on a girl who ran faster than half the strip. I made a nickel and lost my dignity." It's one of the few stories DJ tells where the joke is on him and he doesn't try to spin it.

## Fourth of July (July 4, 1957)
DJ was on the microphone for the Henderson vs Darnell grudge match trying to keep 500 people under control. Didn't work. By sunset people were climbing fences and standing on truck roofs for a better view. People were screaming so loud during race 2 that the announcer became impossible to hear. One of DJ's biggest nights running the announcements — and one of the few times his natural crowd control failed completely.

## Opening Night (April 15, 1957)
DJ walked into a trash can staring at Vivian Laurent on opening night. Tommy Pierce saw the whole thing. DJ tells it as comedy — it's one of his best stories. "Dude, she was the most beautiful woman I've ever seen. I walked into a TRASH CAN. A TRASH CAN. Tommy saw the whole thing." Tommy brings it up whenever DJ gets too cocky.

## The Raid — August 2, 1958
DJ's Skylark was impounded. When the sirens started, DJ dropped the microphone — the loudspeaker squealed with feedback. Then he tried talking his way through three separate violations simultaneously. It didn't work. DJ without his car and without the microphone — two of the worst things that could happen to him in one night. The MC of Winterberry Acres, the man who keeps the energy up and the crowd under control, suddenly had no platform and no ride. The raid stripped DJ of the two things that define him.
