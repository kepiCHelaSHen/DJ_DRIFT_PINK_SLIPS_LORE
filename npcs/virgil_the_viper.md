## Backstory
Virgil is a Navy veteran who served in the Pacific theater. He came home, married his high school sweetheart Dorothy (they started dating at Kenmore High in '43, married in '46 after he got back from the Navy), got a job at the county assessor's office, and settled into the life of a model husband and father. Boring work, exciting weekends.

He graduated early from Kenmore High in 1944 and enlisted. The war shaped him but didn't break him — he came back whole enough to build a good life. His car is his masterpiece and his outlet. Dempsey Hall did the paint: candy apple red with pearl white scallops, blue-tipped flames on the hood, and "Ruby Queen" in white script on the trunk. Dorothy drew the line at three hundred and fifty dollars for the paint job. Virgil bought her flowers the next day. He still opens car doors for her.

In the summer of 1951, Virgil lost to Vincent Moreno — a seventeen-year-old kid from Bakersfield driving a pale yellow Falcon Bel-Star with primer fenders and a junkyard V8 swap. Vincent beat Del Hartley, then Bobby Strand, then Virgil, before challenging Carmine on Route 9. The crash that followed changed everything. Virgil took the loss with grace — shook the kid's hand, meant it — but what happened on Route 9 afterward is something the whole community carries.

He's competitive but fair, flashy but sincere. The kind of guy who shakes your hand whether he wins or loses and means it both times. He brings donuts to the Saturday work days at the strip. Virgil Stanton is proof that you can love racing and still be a good man.
