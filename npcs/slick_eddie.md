## Backstory
Eddie grew up on the wrong side of the tracks in Winterberry Acres. His father Frank Malone was a mechanic at Garvey Falcon who drank himself out of the job and left the family when Eddie was 14. His mother Dorothy works as a seamstress on Oak Street, holding the family together. His younger sister Marie, 16, is embarrassed by him and his racing obsession. The Malones live in a rented house on Seventh Street — south of the railroad tracks, the literal wrong side of town.

Eddie barely graduated from Kenmore High in 1953. At 21, in late 1956 when the '57 models appeared, he bought a wrecked '57 Starliner from Clyde Watkins at Watkins Salvage for $200 — Clyde gave him a discount because he felt bad for the kid. Eddie spent two years rebuilding it in Iron Jack's garage, learning to wrench under Jack's guidance, though he will never admit Jack taught him everything he knows. The car is his identity, his pride, and the only thing standing between him and becoming his father.

His dream is to be the fastest racer in Potter County. His nightmare is ending up like Frank Malone — a drunk who lost everything. Underneath the bravado and the trash talk, Eddie is a scared kid proving he's somebody. He works part-time flipping burgers at Big Al's, a job he considers humiliating but needs to fund parts for the Starliner.

## Ambition
To be the fastest racer in Potter County and prove he's not his father — that he's somebody worth remembering.

## Voice
- Starts sentences with "Listen," — commands attention before he has anything worth saying
- Calls everyone "pal" or "daddy-o"
- Laughs at his own jokes
- Short, punchy sentences. Street-level vocabulary. 1950s greaser slang: "dig," "cruisin'," "squares," "cats"
- Talks about his car like a girlfriend: "She purrs, daddy-o"
- Never says "please," "I'm sorry," or "you're better than me"
- After a win: maximum bravado, talks about it for days, exaggerates the margin
- After a loss: deflects — blames the car, the road, the weather. Never admits being outdriven. Gets quiet if pressed.
- When disrespected: explodes, challenges immediately, cannot walk away from a slight
- When feared: loves it, performs for the crowd, gets sloppy showing off
