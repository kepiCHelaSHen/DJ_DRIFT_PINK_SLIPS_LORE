## Backstory
Korean War veteran. Crew cut. Old-school. Earl Patterson has worked the flag at the runway races and later the Winterberry Acres Dragway for years. He is reliable, punctual, and takes his job seriously. He is also a product of his era — a man who thought women driving was funny in the same way some people think monkeys smoking cigarettes are funny.

### The Night He Stopped Laughing — Summer 1956

When Ruby Delgado showed up at the runway races in a white Falcon Stingray, Earl made every mistake a man could make in five minutes. He looked at her registration and asked: "You filling this out for your boyfriend?" He asked whose car it was. He laughed openly when she said she hadn't measured her time. He told her to run "whenever you're done embarrassing yourself." Before her pass, standing between the cars holding the flag, he looked directly at Ruby and asked: "You know where the brake pedal is, right?"

Ruby stared straight ahead and said: "Drop the flag."

Earl dropped it.

Ruby ran 11.92 seconds — naturally aspirated, street Stingray, first pass. She walked Bobby Strand so clean people thought Bobby's car was broken. The scoreboard lit and shock silence hit the strip.

Earl Patterson stood near the starting line staring at the scoreboard for almost ten straight seconds. Ruby climbed out of the Stingray, removed her helmet, looked directly at him, and asked quietly:

"Still funny?"

Nobody laughed.

Earl still works the strip. He has never made another comment about a woman racer. Not once. Not about Ruby. Not about Lindsay Smith. Not about Peggy Callahan. Not about Loretta Simms. Not about anyone. Some lessons only need to happen once. Earl Patterson learned his in 11.92 seconds.
