## Backstory
Nicky Ferris made more money the night of the Johnny Vale vs Henderson race than he's ever admitted. He ran the book on all four races. The odds shifted dramatically after the first loss — Johnny went from heavy favorite to even money after race two, and by race four, the smart money had already moved to Henderson. Nicky read the room faster than anyone and adjusted the line accordingly. It was the night that established his reputation as the sharpest book in Potter County.

The night Tony Marcone walked into the pool hall changed the direction of Nicky's life. It was 1950, a rainstorm, past midnight. Nicky was counting money when the front bell jingled. A tired-looking man in a soaked overcoat and polished shoes walked in carrying a small leather suitcase. Nicky looked up once and knew immediately — Tony wasn't local, and Tony wasn't somebody you lied to. After Tony put Charlie Blevins on the floor without raising his voice, Tony looked at Nicky and asked: "Who handles business in this town?" Nicky should have lied. Instead he answered: "Depends what kinda business." That exchange defined the next seven years. Nicky understood numbers. Tony understood fear. They built the quietest criminal operation in Potter County together.

Nobody knows Nicky's family situation. He has a sister in Philadelphia he calls once a month. Keeps odd hours. Came west to escape something in Philadelphia that he never discusses. He's educated — speaks well, knows mathematics, could have been an accountant. Instead he runs the book on every race at the strip. He sets fair odds and pays promptly. His operation is honest by bookie standards. He takes 10% of every pot — his vigorish. On a busy night he clears a hundred dollars. He's the richest man at the strip who nobody envies because his money comes with a reputation that keeps people at arm's length. Did not attend Kenmore High — from Philadelphia, arrived in 1950.

## Burden
Whatever he's running from in Philadelphia. He never discusses it. His wealth comes with a reputation that keeps people at arm's length — he's the richest man at the strip who nobody envies. But the deeper burden is this: Nicky chose this. Nobody forced him. When Tony walked into the pool hall that night, Nicky could have lied, could have said he didn't know anything, could have pointed Tony somewhere else. Instead he answered honestly and became Tony's partner. That's the part that keeps him up at night — not that Tony found him, but that Nicky invited it.

## Fourth of July (July 4, 1957)
Nicky took more bets that night than any three weekends combined. The odds shifted dramatically after race 1 — when Darnell beat Henderson clean by a full car length, the smart money scrambled. Henderson went from heavy favorite to near-even for race 2. By the time pink slips went up for race 3, money was changing hands everywhere and Deputy Morris couldn't do a thing about it. One of Nicky's biggest nights.

## October 1958 — The Biggest Pink Slip Race
Nicky ran out of paper recording side bets. The biggest action he'd ever seen — bigger than opening night, bigger than the Fourth of July. Tony took bets personally from the tower. Money was changing hands everywhere. When Henderson gave Leon's title back, the payouts got complicated — but Nicky's book was already closed. The vigorish was collected. Biggest single-night take in the history of the Winterberry Acres book.

## Opening Night (April 15, 1957)
Nicky made more money on opening night than any night before or since. He ran the book on all races. When the stranger appeared with Vivian Laurent and challenged Henderson, Nicky moved Henderson's odds from 4-to-1 favorite to 3-to-1. "Should've gone even." The gate money for that single race was the biggest pot Winterberry Acres had ever seen, and Nicky had a piece of every dollar that changed hands.

## The Raid — August 2, 1958
The gambling was one of the triggers for the raid. Nicky's operation had gotten too big and too visible — betting everywhere, money changing hands openly, the county unable to ignore it any longer. Nicky wasn't arrested that night, but his book was part of the reason the county decided to act. The church groups, the county commissioners, the parents — they all pointed at the gambling alongside the racing. Nicky's operation had outgrown the shadows it needed to survive in. After the raid, Nicky would need to get quieter or get caught.
