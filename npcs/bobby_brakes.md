## Backstory
Single mother raised him — Dolores Strand works at the phone company. Father left when Bobby was 3. No siblings. In the summer of 1951, Bobby lost to Vincent Moreno — a seventeen-year-old kid from Bakersfield driving a pale yellow Falcon Bel-Star with primer fenders and a junkyard V8 swap. Vincent beat Del Hartley, then Bobby, then Virgil Stanton, before challenging Carmine on Route 9. The crash that followed changed everything. Bobby doesn't dwell on the loss, but it happened.

In the summer of 1956, Bobby was Ruby Delgado's first opponent at the runway races. He leaned out his window in staging and yelled "Try not to stall it, sweetheart!" Ruby ran 11.92 and beat him so badly people genuinely thought something had broken on the Fury. Nothing had. She walked him clean. It is a humiliation Bobby has never lived down. He was the punchline of the night the strip learned not to laugh at Ruby Delgado.

Bobby is the cautionary tale of talent without discipline. He's fast — genuinely fast — but he spends every dollar on parts and bets instead of rent and savings. His Fury runs low thirteens but the brakes squeal, the tires are bald, and the seat belt was held on with a wire hanger until Jack Drummond refused to let him race without fixing it. Bobby fixed the belt. Nothing else. His girlfriend Nancy Wheeler is learning to drive his car.

## The Christmas Eve Truce (Christmas Eve, 1956)

Bobby apologized to Tommy Pierce on Christmas Eve for wrecking his spare transmission. Tommy admitted it was partly his fault. Nobody fully believed either apology, but that wasn't the point. Christmas Eve at Mel's was the one night where things like that could happen.

## The Raid — August 2, 1958
Bobby's Fury was impounded. Bobby screamed at an inspector and nearly got arrested. Bobby's temper at its worst — the cautionary tale of talent without discipline, losing his car because he couldn't keep his mouth shut any better than he could keep his brakes maintained.
