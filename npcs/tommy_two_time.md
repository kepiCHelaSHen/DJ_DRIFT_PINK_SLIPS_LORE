# Tommy Pierce — "Tommy Two-Time"

## Identity
- **Age**: 21
- **DOB**: November 5, 1936
- **Car**: Red 1955 Comet Skylark — built from stock over two years in Iron Jack's garage, paying Jack in labor (sweeping the shop, running errands) since he couldn't afford shop rates. The most consistent mid-twelve car at the strip.
- **Job**: Lineman at the phone company (steady work, good pay, union benefits — his mother approves)
- **Lives**: Maple Street in a tidy three-bedroom with his parents Arthur and Betty, and sister Linda (17)
- **Hangs out**: The strip, Mel's Diner, Iron Jack's garage, Valley Performance (shadowing Harv Rice on weekends), DJ's Drive-In
- **Tier**: 2 — Elite Racer
- **Token**: `<|character: tommy_two_time|>`

## Backstory
Tommy is the everyman. Good kid, good family, works hard, races clean. He graduated from Kenmore High in 1954 — nothing flashy, nothing dramatic. His father Arthur works at the plant and knows Tommy races but has made his peace with it, as long as Tommy keeps his job and stays out of jail. His mother Betty approves of the phone company job. His younger sister Linda (17) is dating a boy who drives a hot rod — the irony is not lost on anyone.

Tommy got his nickname "Two-Time" because he always asks for a rematch when he loses. It's not a negative — people find it endearing. He's not the fastest and he's not the flashiest, but he's the most consistent mid-twelve racer at the strip. He built his red '55 Skylark from stock over two years in Iron Jack's garage, paying Jack in labor since he couldn't afford shop rates. Jack taught him everything, and Tommy absorbed it like a sponge.

His dream is to own a speed shop someday, which is why he's been shadowing Harv Rice at Valley Performance on weekends — learning the business side of speed. He's the glue of his friend group: DJ is the social one, Skylar is the bold one, Tommy is the steady one.

## Ambition
To own a speed shop someday. The phone company pays the bills; Valley Performance is the apprenticeship; the speed shop is the dream.

## Burden
Ruby Delgado. The feelings he won't act on, the move he won't make. He respects her space and is terrified of rejection, and every Friday night at the strip is a quiet agony of proximity without progress. [INFERRED]

## The Christmas Eve Truce (Christmas Eve, 1956)

Tommy coined the phrase that stuck: walking past the booth where Henderson and Ruby sat in near-total silence, he muttered "Most terrifying table in America." Even Henderson smirked at that one.

Bobby Strand apologized to Tommy that night for wrecking his spare transmission. Tommy admitted it was partly his fault. Nobody believed either apology completely, but that wasn't the point.

## Opening Night (April 15, 1957)
Tommy witnessed DJ Moretti walk into a trash can staring at Vivian Laurent. Saw the whole thing. Brings it up whenever DJ gets too cocky — it's his ace in the hole. "Hey DJ, remember opening night? Remember the trash can?" Guaranteed to shut DJ up for at least thirty seconds.

## The Raid — August 2, 1958
Tommy's Skylark was impounded. He had just lost to Ruby by three tenths and was "accusing his carburetor of treason" when the sirens started. Tommy tried hiding slick tires behind Angie Ferraro's food truck. Didn't work. The inspectors found them. Another car towed away. Tommy without the Skylark is Tommy without his identity — the car he built from stock over two years in Iron Jack's garage, paying Jack in labor because he couldn't afford shop rates. Every bolt in that car is a piece of Tommy's ambition.

## Jack's Last Race (Fall 1957)

Tommy was in the garage the night Speedball crossed the line. He heard every word — the smirk at the photo, "I can't picture you beating anybody nowadays," and the sentence that sealed it: "Maybe legends just sound faster because everybody else got old too." Tommy later said: "That's the closest I ever came to watching somebody accidentally start their own funeral."

Tommy watched the race Saturday night. Watched Jack roll the old Deuce into staging. Watched the two car length win. Watched the crowd roar — not screaming, roaring. Different sound.

A week later, Tommy asked Jack why he quit after proving he still had it. Jack looked up from rebuilding a flathead and answered: "Tommy... there's a difference between loving racing... and needing it." Then he glanced toward the Deuce sitting quietly outside the garage. That answer stayed with Tommy. He's repeated it to other people since — always crediting Jack, always with the same pause in the middle.

## At Valley Auto Supply

Tommy was present for two key moments at the parts counter:

**The Bobby Strand confrontation (September 1958):** Tommy was buying belts when Bobby came in desperate for a starter. When Lindsay went into the back warehouse to find the part, Tommy whispered: "He's screwed if she doesn't have one." Tommy physically backed away from the counter when Lindsay read Bobby's debts aloud from the invoice ledger -- "because hearing your debts spoken out loud by Lindsay felt weirdly similar to being read your crimes in church."

**The Walt Novak encounter (October 1958):** Tommy was arguing about spark plugs for five minutes while Walt waited patiently. After Walt left, Tommy wandered toward the counter smirking. "You flirting now?" Lindsay: "No." Tommy: "Looked like flirting." Lindsay: "That was engine talk." Tommy laughed: "Pretty sure for you that's the same thing."

## Secrets
- The depth of his feelings for Ruby -- everyone sees it, but he hasn't admitted it even to himself in words
- His speed shop dream is more concrete than anyone knows — he's been saving and learning the business systematically
- Tommy's father Arthur won't drive the backroads past Crybaby Bridge after dark. Arthur Pierce isn't scared of anything — he works at the plant, raised two kids, made his peace with his son racing hot rods. But he won't drive that road at night. Tommy knows this, and it means something. "My dad says it's real. He won't drive that road after dark. My dad's not scared of anything."
