## Backstory
Nobody called him Lucky because he was fortunate. They called him Lucky because he survived things that should've killed him.

Korean War veteran. Card cheat. Occasional mechanic. Occasional criminal. Permanent gambler.

Leon rolled into Winterberry Acres three weeks before the biggest pink slip race in the town's history, towing a metallic gold Falcon Starliner behind a battered Atlas Bulldog tractor. The car was unbelievable — fuel-injected 283, close-ratio gearbox, hand-fabricated headers, lightened body. Purpose-built.

His first night at the strip, he posted an eleven-flat. His second night, an eleven-oh-two. By the third weekend people started whispering something dangerous: maybe Henderson finally found somebody faster.

Leon owed Tony Marcone money. Tony already owned part of Leon's future. Jack Drummond saw it immediately — the twitchiness in the staging lanes, the smoking, the eyes that kept moving. "That boy owes somebody money." If Leon lost the Starliner, he had absolutely nothing left to bargain with. That kind of pressure changes how men drive.

The challenge happened publicly at Mel's Diner. Packed counter, Saturday afternoon. Leon sat three stools down from Henderson drinking coffee like he didn't have survival instincts. "You still race for titles, or just stories nowadays?" Henderson laid the Drifter's title beside Leon's pink slip. The diner erupted.

October 18, 1958. Cold night, dry air. The most beautiful pairing Winterberry Acres had ever seen — black Drifter, gold Starliner. Perfect launch from both cars. Dead even through half-track. Two hundred feet from the finish, Henderson inches ahead. Then Leon glanced sideways. Just once. Lost the groove. The Starliner snapped sideways, hit the barrier nose-first, showered sparks, spun twice.

Concussion. Broken collarbone. Broken wrist. Lucky again.

The Starliner wasn't lucky. Front end destroyed, frame twisted, engine halfway out of the mounts. Totaled.

Henderson sprinted back before safety crews even moved. Then — while Leon sat on the ambulance bumper holding a towel against his forehead — Henderson handed the title back: "Because whatever owns you already took enough." He looked at Tony Marcone when he said it.

## Ambition
To win big enough to get out from under whatever owns him. Leon is a gambler — not for the thrill, but because gambling is the only way men like him know how to change their circumstances fast enough to matter.

## Burden
The debt. Tony Marcone. The things Leon did in Korea and after that gave him the name "Lucky." The gold Starliner was supposed to be his way out. Now it's scrap metal.
