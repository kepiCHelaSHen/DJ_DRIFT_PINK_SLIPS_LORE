## Backstory
The Yamamotos opened The Frost Top in 1948 after arriving in Winterberry Acres with two suitcases, a coffee tin full of savings, and a silence about the war so complete it became part of the building itself. Before 1942, the family owned a fruit stand outside the valley. Then came the camps. Mrs. Yamamoto never forgot the sound of boots near the front porch. The neighbors' silence afterward.

She decided one thing in the camp: if they survived, she would build one place nobody could take from her again. That's why the rules matter so much. Safety matters. Dignity matters. Respect matters.

Mrs. Yamamoto runs the front of the Frost Top. The place sits three blocks off Main Street beneath a faded cherry-red sign that buzzes softly at night. It smells like vanilla syrup, grilled onions, floor polish, cigarette smoke trapped in curtains, cherry phosphate — and safety. That matters. Especially for girls tired of being watched constantly everywhere else.

The women of Winterberry Acres understood what the Frost Top actually was: neutral ground. No fighting. No grabbing. No humiliating girls in public. No strip politics crossing the front door. Mrs. Yamamoto enforced those rules with terrifying efficiency despite being barely five feet tall.

"You girls deserve one place in this town where you don't have to negotiate."

That line became law at the Frost Top.

### The Afternoon She Threw Ronnie Dubowski Out (Spring 1958)
Saturday. Warm weather. Every booth full. Ronnie Dubowski arrived drunk and started bothering Bonnie Carmichael — leaning too close, unwanted touching, talking louder after being ignored. The women exchanged looks. Tiny signals. Shared tension. Then Ronnie reached for Bonnie's notebook.

Mrs. Yamamoto emerged from behind the counter almost instantly. "Put it back." Ronnie smirked. "Put. It. Back." People swore the temperature dropped ten degrees. Every woman in the restaurant watched him silently. Not scared. Evaluating. Ronnie tossed the notebook back carelessly. Mrs. Yamamoto nodded toward the door. "Not today." Lindsay spoke from her booth without looking up: "Almost like women enjoy eating without dealing with idiots." The whole restaurant laughed. Ronnie pointed at Lindsay: "You race one car and suddenly you think you're somebody." Mrs. Yamamoto walked to the front door, held it open. "Out." Ronnie left fast.

### The Milkshake Pact (Summer 1959)
When the girls made their pact — no shrinking, no quitting jobs for husbands, no pretending they're stupid — Mrs. Yamamoto overheard from behind the counter. She quietly walked over carrying six fresh milkshakes. Set them on the table. "Good rule."

### The Night Ruby Came Inside (1959)
Ruby Delgado parked outside the Frost Top for nearly two years but rarely came in. Cars were armor. After a brutal race weekend, Ruby sat alone in the Stingray after midnight with the engine off and hands still gripping the wheel. Mrs. Yamamoto appeared beside the driver-side window. Tiny knock on the glass. Held out a paper bag. "Food." Ruby: "I'm fine." Mrs. Yamamoto: "No you're not." Then: "You coming inside or planning to live in this car forever?" Ruby followed her inside. Mrs. Yamamoto brought coffee without asking.
