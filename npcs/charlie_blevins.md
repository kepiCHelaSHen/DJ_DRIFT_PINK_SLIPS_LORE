## Backstory
Charlie Blevins was the local tough guy at the pool hall on Vine Street — a mean drunk, a factory worker, the kind of man who mistook silence for weakness and size for authority. That ended in 1950 when a stranger walked into the pool hall during a rainstorm carrying a leather suitcase.

Charlie made the mistake of getting curious. He wandered over half-loaded on whiskey, slapped the counter, asked too many questions, and reached for the stranger's suitcase. Nobody ever figured out exactly what happened next. Only that Charlie suddenly hit the floor hard enough to crack the leg of a chair. The stranger — Tony Marcone — never raised his voice, never looked angry, never even stood fully upright. He just leaned down and said: "Now you know enough."

Charlie still lives in Winterberry Acres. Still works at the factory. Still drinks at the pool hall. But he flinches when Tony walks in. He is a cautionary tale nobody talks about because Charlie won't talk about it either. He was the first man in town to learn what Tony Marcone was — and the lesson has kept him quiet for seven years.

## Burden
The memory of being put down without effort by a man who didn't even need to stand up. The knowledge that everyone in the pool hall saw it. The silence that followed — not from the room, but from Charlie himself, for seven years and counting.
