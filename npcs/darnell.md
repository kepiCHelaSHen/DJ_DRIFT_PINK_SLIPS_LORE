## Backstory
Darnell is the mystery. He shows up from Riverside in a black '55 Falcon Bel-Star. He runs fast, leaves fast, and doesn't talk. He ran an eleven-four — the fastest time ever recorded at the Acres. Nobody knows his story. Clarence Moody (#95) knows something about him but won't say. He called Rockin' Ray Reardon about "Darnell." Rumors range from "he's a professional ringer" to "he's running from the law" to "he's just a kid with a fast car." The truth, when it comes out, will surprise everyone. His rivalry with Henderson Cole (#9) is the defining conflict of the current racing season — Henderson won their Fourth of July grudge match 2-1 but it rattled him.

The first time Darnell ran an eleven-four at the Acres, the entire strip went silent. Even Carmine stopped writing time slips. Word spread across the region in under two weeks. Racers from Riverside, Greenfield, Millbury, even Bakersfield started showing up just to watch the tension build. By July 4th the whole county knew the grudge match was happening.

July 4, 1957. Five hundred spectators. Best two out of three. Pink slips on the third run. Darnell accepted the pink slip terms instantly, without hesitation — the part that scared Carmine most, because men who hesitate can still be reasoned with.

Race 1: Darnell's Bel-Star pulled steadily from half-track and crossed first by nearly a full car length. The crowd exploded — half cheering, half horrified. Nobody beat Henderson clean. When Henderson accused him of sandbagging, Darnell replied: "You finally raced somebody honest."

Race 2: Henderson got mean — San Bernardino instincts, hard burnout, aggressive staging. Henderson edged Darnell by half a hood. The closest race Winterberry Acres had ever seen.

Race 3: Pink slips. Before the final race, Carmine grabbed Darnell's arm: "You don't have to prove this." Darnell answered something quietly. Carmine let go and looked genuinely disturbed. What Darnell said is unknown. The race staged at 10:42 PM. Perfect launch from both cars. Half-track — even. Three-quarter track — Henderson starts pulling. Then Darnell's Bel-Star twitched. Tiny movement, almost invisible. The car drifted toward the wall, corrected, lost momentum. Henderson crossed first by less than half a car length.

Darnell coasted to the shutdown area slowly. Too slowly. He handed Henderson the pink slip without argument, without emotion. Henderson looked at the paper, looked at the car, looked at Darnell — and handed the title back. "Keep the car." Darnell asked why. Henderson answered: "Because if you'd stayed in it another second, you were dead." Darnell folded the title, put it back in his jacket, got into the Bel-Star, and disappeared west down Route 9 before the trophy ceremony even started. Gone. Like always.
