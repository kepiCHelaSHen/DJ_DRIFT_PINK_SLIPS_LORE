## Town Overview

**Winterberry Acres** is a small town in the American heartland, population approximately 10,000. Set in a wide valley surrounded by low hills and farmland, the town sits at the crossroads of Route 9 (the main highway connecting to the city) and Route 4 (a two-lane road running east toward open country). Founded in the 1890s as an agricultural community, Winterberry Acres grew steadily through the war years as a nearby aircraft factory brought workers to the region. By the mid-1950s the factory has closed but the town remains — a working-class community of mechanics, plant workers, farmers, and shopkeepers bound together by Friday night football, Sunday church, and the unmistakable sound of engines on the strip.

**Era:** 1949-1959 (the town's racing history; the player arrives in 1959)
**Setting:** Heartland USA (deliberately unanchored from any real state)
**County:** Potter County
**Nearby towns:** Millbury (20 miles east), Greenfield (15 miles south), Riverside (45 miles west — the "big city")

## Geography & Streets

### Main Roads
| Road | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Main Street** | The spine of downtown. Runs north-south through the center of town. Mel's Diner, Valley Auto Supply, the Rialto Theater, and most shops are here. |
| **Route 9** | The highway. Runs east-west through the north edge of town. Fast road, two lanes, connects to Riverside. The curve past the Miller farm is notorious. |
| **Route 4** | Secondary highway heading east toward the desert and the dry lakes. The grain elevator marks where Route 4 leaves town. |
| **Airport Road** | County road south of town leading to the old airfield. This is where the strip is. |
| **Old Cannery Road** | Scenic back road along the creek. Where the old fruit cannery used to be. Popular for cruising. |
| **Miller Road** | Runs south to the county line. Named after the Miller farming family. Unpaved past the last intersection. |

## Timeline of Key Events

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| 1942 | Jack Drummond runs moonshine for his uncle. |
| 1945 | Jack and Gene Hartwell return from the Pacific. Start racing on back roads. |
| 1947 | First organized race on the old airport runway. Gene, Jack, and a handful of vets. |
| 1950 | **Tony Marcone arrives in Winterberry Acres** during a rainstorm. Parks a black Atlas Box truck behind the pool hall on Vine Street just after midnight. Puts Charlie Blevins on the floor. Meets Nicky Ferris. Within a week, maps every debt, poker game, vulnerable deputy, and cheating businessman in Potter County. Partners with Nicky Ferris. The quietest criminal operation in county history begins. |
| 1949 (spring) | Carmine Ferraro starts racing his Comet Drifter. Abe Prescott builds his roadster. |
| 1949 (August) | **The Crybaby Bridge crash.** Two racers approach Miller's Crossing from opposite directions. One goes through the guardrail into the ravine. No body is ever found. Jack and Gene climb into the ravine that night. Carmine is on the backroads and hears the crash. Wes tows the wreck. The bridge is renamed Crybaby Bridge. This event plants the seed for organized, off-street racing. |
| 1951 (summer) | **The Route 9 accident.** Carmine Ferraro (31) races Vincent Moreno (17, Bakersfield) on Route 9 near Miller Farm. Vincent — driving a pale yellow Falcon Bel-Star with primer fenders and a junkyard V8 swap — had already beaten Del Hartley, Bobby Strand, and Virgil Stanton. ~30 witnesses, headlights off. Vincent's car hits gravel on the downhill bend, launches into a sycamore tree. Vincent survives with broken spine, crushed pelvis, collapsed lung, blind in one eye. Carmine quits racing that night. Jack, Gene, and Wes had all warned him not to race. |
| 1957 (late) | **Vincent Moreno dies** from complications tied to the 1951 Route 9 crash injuries. Carmine attends the funeral alone — nobody knows, not even Angie. Afterward, Carmine drives to the empty airport runway and sits alone in the dark. The dragway mission crystallizes fully. |
| 1953 (late) | Henderson Cole arrives from San Bernardino towing a black Drifter behind a dented Atlas pickup. Beats Johnny Vale four straight in a single night. Johnny crashes on Route 9 hours later — takes the curve past Miller Farm too fast, rolls twice, hits a sycamore. Dead before dawn. Henderson becomes king of the strip. Jack Drummond buys the Rocket 88 wreck from Clyde Watkins and rebuilds it over six months. |
| Late 1956 | Eddie Malone buys a wrecked '57 Starliner at 21, rebuilds it over the winter. |
| 1956 (spring) | Carmine takes over organizing the races on Airport Road. Introduces the Christmas tree and entry fees. |
| 1956 (summer) | **Ruby Delgado arrives at the strip for the first time.** Runs 11.92 in a naturally aspirated white Falcon Stingray — her father Miguel's unfinished project, completed months after his death. Flagman Earl Patterson laughs. Bobby Strand yells "Try not to stall it, sweetheart!" Ruby walks Bobby clean, stares at Earl, and asks: "Still funny?" Nobody laughs again. Henderson Cole is the only person who didn't laugh — walks over after, asks who built the motor. Ruby: "My father started it. I finished it." Henderson smiles for the first time in years. |
| 1956 (December 24) | **Christmas Eve 1956: The Christmas Truce.** Snow falls on Winterberry Acres — the only snow in years. Every racer in Potter County gathers at Mel's. No beef. No betting. No business. Even Tony Marcone respects the truce. Henderson sits with people voluntarily for the only time ever. Ruby sits across from him. Gene closes the night: "Tomorrow you idiots can go back to trying to kill each other." Henderson: "Yeah." |
| 1957 (spring) | Valley Street Rod Association proposes a sanctioned drag strip to the county. Approved 3-2. |
| 1957 (April 15) | **Winterberry Acres Dragway opens.** Ribbon cut by Gene Hartwell. Free hot dogs. Vivian Laurent arrives from Riverside with a mysterious driver in a dark green Falcon Starliner. She challenges Henderson. Henderson wins by a hood length. The stranger's hands are bleeding — he vanishes into the dark and is never seen again. Vivian becomes a legend. |
| 1957 (July 4) | **Henderson vs Darnell Price (Riverside Kid).** 500+ spectators. Best 2 of 3. Henderson wins 2-1. Darnell wins race 1 by a car length — Henderson's first clean loss since Johnny Vale. Henderson wins race 2 by half a hood — meanest anyone has ever seen him. Race 3 — pink slips — Darnell's Bel-Star twitches near the finish, Henderson wins by half a car length. Henderson hands the pink slip back: "Keep the car." "Because if you'd stayed in it another second, you were dead." Darnell disappears west on Route 9. The third time a car has twitched at a critical moment: Vincent Moreno (1951), the Stranger's Starliner (opening night), Darnell's Bel-Star (Fourth of July). Carmine witnessed all three. |
| 1957 (fall) | **Jack Drummond's last race.** Speedball (Dennis Kohl) wins six straight, gets cocky, disrespects Jack in his own garage, insults the old racing photo. Jack challenges him. Saturday night — the strip overflows. Henderson shows up early, tells Carmine "Kid already lost." Jack races the old '34 Comet Deuce — his original hot rod from 1948, not the Rocket 88. Wins by two car lengths. Speedball apologizes: "I was outta line." Jack: "Yeah." Speedball: "How the hell are you still that fast?" Jack: "Because I stopped trying to prove I was fast a long time ago." Jack never races seriously again. "There's a difference between loving racing and needing it." The Deuce sits outside the garage — proof the old stories were real. |
| 1957 (fall) | NHRA regional event announced for the Dragway. The strip goes on the map. |
| 1958 (August 2) | **The Raid.** Deputy Morris leads county and state police to the dragway. Four sheriff cruisers, two state police cars, a paddy wagon, an inspection truck. Triggered by the Miller boy (16, wrapped his brother's Bel-Star around a telephone pole after leaving the strip) plus mounting pressure from church groups, county commissioners, and Sheriff Donnelly. 12 cars impounded including Bobby's Fury, Tommy's Skylark, DJ's Skylark, Ruby's Stingray (technical violation). Henderson's Drifter NOT impounded — Morris found a Vincent Moreno newspaper clipping on the passenger seat and changed his mind. Carmine confronts Morris: "You shut this place down and they go back to Route 9." Morris: "And if I DON'T shut it down, somebody dies here instead." Winterberry Acres stops feeling invincible. The outside world noticed them. |
| 1958 (October 18) | **The Biggest Pink Slip Race.** Henderson Cole vs Leon "Lucky" Serrano — Korean War vet, card cheat, gambler who arrived three weeks earlier towing a metallic gold Falcon Starliner behind an Atlas Bulldog. Leon posted an eleven-flat. Challenge happened publicly at Mel's Diner — Leon tossed the pink slip, Henderson laid the Drifter's title beside it. Dead even race until Leon glanced sideways, lost the groove, crashed into the barrier. Concussion, broken collarbone, broken wrist. Car totaled. Henderson gave the title back: "Because whatever owns you already took enough." Looked at Tony Marcone when he said it. Tony visibly angry for the first and only time in public. Henderson's second act of mercy (after Darnell on the Fourth of July). Origin of the Henderson vs. Marcone tension. |
| 1959 | **Game starts.** Player arrives in Winterberry Acres. All events 1949-1958 are history the player discovers through conversations. |

## Usage Notes for Corpus Generation

1. **Every dialogue should reference Winterberry Acres locations by name.** Not "the diner" — "Mel's." Not "the gas station" — "Wes's Texaco." Not "the parts store" — "Valley Auto Supply."
2. **NPC names should appear in dialogue.** When characters gossip, they gossip about named people. "Did you hear what Cooter did?" not "Did you hear what some guy did?"
3. **Streets should be referenced by name.** "He lives over on Seventh Street" not "he lives on the wrong side of town."
4. **The strip should sometimes be called by name.** "Winterberry Acres Dragway" in formal contexts, "the Acres" or "the strip" in casual talk.
5. **Nearby towns should be referenced.** Millbury for nearby errands, Greenfield for the used car lot, Riverside for the big city.
6. **The timeline should be respected.** Jack is old school. Eddie is young. Gene remembers when there was nothing. Carmine carries the weight of the accident.
