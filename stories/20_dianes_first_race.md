# Diane's First Real Race

By late summer of 1959, everybody at Winterberry Acres knew Diane Kowalski could drive.

The secret was long dead by then.

Mostly because Tommy Pierce couldn't keep his mouth shut about anything for more than forty-five seconds.

Still, people underestimated her constantly.

Not because she lacked skill.

Because Diane didn't LOOK like a racer.

She still worked mornings at Mel's.
Still tied her hair back with diner scarves.
Still smelled faintly like coffee and fryer grease half the time.

People confused softness for weakness constantly around Winterberry Acres.

Diane learned to let them.

The race that changed everything happened during Labor Day weekend.

Huge crowd.
Packed stands.
Warm air carrying smoke and music across the valley.

Diane wasn't even supposed to race that night.

She was helping Mel cover the diner rush when Bobby Strand stormed into Mel's furious after blowing a clutch during eliminations.

"Whole damn weekend ruined."

Diane looked up from refilling ketchup bottles.

"What happened?"

"Transmission launch chatter."
"Car's dead."

Tommy Pierce immediately started laughing.

"Sounds like operator error."

Bobby threw a napkin at him.

Then Tommy looked toward Diane suddenly.

Dangerous look.

"Oh no," Diane said immediately.

Tommy grinned.

"Oh yes."

Twenty minutes later Diane found herself sitting inside Bobby's black Comet Fury while half the strip gathered near staging laughing at the insanity unfolding.

Bobby paced nearby nervously.

"You bend it, you bought it."

Diane:
"With what money?"

Fair point.

Mel stood near the fence looking deeply unhappy about all of this.

Ruby stood beside him oddly calm.

That mattered.

Because Ruby only got calm when she already knew the outcome.

The matchup was supposed to be harmless:
Diane versus Buddy Crane in his stock Skylark.

Just a fun run.

Crowd entertainment.

Buddy made the mistake immediately.

Leaning out the window:
"Try not to spill coffee in there, sweetheart."

Whole crowd laughed.

Diane just tightened her gloves.

Ruby noticed the expression change instantly.

Then quietly told Mel:
"He's dead."

Flag dropped.

Buddy launched hard.

Too hard.

Rear wheels spun.

Diane launched clean.

Smooth.
Controlled.
Beautiful shift into second.

By half-track she already had him.

By the finish line she'd embarrassed him completely.

The crowd exploded laughing while Buddy crossed second looking spiritually damaged.

Diane coasted back stunned.

Not because she won.

Because now she understood the feeling.

The sound.
The focus.
The silence Henderson described years earlier at Mel's.

Everything disappearing except the road ahead.

She climbed out near staging while racers surrounded the Fury yelling and laughing.

Mel walked over slowly.

Diane braced herself.

Instead he looked at the timeslip.

Then:
"Huh."

"What?"

Mel handed it back.

"You shift smoother than Tommy."

Tommy yelled from across the pits:
"HEY."

Nobody listened.

Then Mel looked at Diane quietly for a second.

And for the first time since she started racing…

he stopped looking afraid.

Not completely.

Just enough.

That mattered more than winning ever could.
