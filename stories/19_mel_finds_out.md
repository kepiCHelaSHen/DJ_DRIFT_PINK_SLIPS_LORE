# Mel Finds Out

Mel Kowalski discovered Diane had been practicing at Winterberry Acres the same way all fathers discover terrifying truths:

completely by accident.

Summer, 1958.

Thursday night.
After closing.

Mel was carrying trash behind the diner when he heard an engine rev somewhere near the strip garages.

Normally he ignored it.

Racers practically lived out there after midnight.

But something sounded strange.

The shifting.

Too cautious.
Too measured.

Like somebody learning.

Mel followed the sound through the dark lot toward the garages while floodlights buzzed softly overhead.

Then the Falcon appeared around the corner.

Tommy Pierce's primer-gray coupe.

And behind the wheel—

Diane.

Mel physically stopped walking.

Diane saw him immediately.

Which unfortunately caused her to stall the car in the middle of the lane.

Silence.

Terrible silence.

The Falcon sat dead while both father and daughter stared at each other through the windshield.

Finally Mel walked slowly toward the driver-side window.

Diane considered fleeing to Canada.

Instead she rolled the window down carefully.

Mel looked inside the car.
Then at her.
Then at the strip beyond them.

Finally:
"How long?"

Diane swallowed.

"…a couple months."

Mel closed his eyes briefly.

"Jesus Christ."

That hurt worse than yelling.

Diane immediately started defending herself:
"I'm careful."
"I only practice after closing."
"Tommy said I'm getting good."
"I'm not stupid."

Mel raised one hand.

Instant silence.

Then he quietly asked:
"You think this place needs another person getting hurt?"

That landed hard.

Because Diane knew the stories:

Route 9
Crybaby Bridge
Vincent Moreno
every broken thing racing dragged behind it

But she also knew something else.

Something Mel didn't want to admit.

"I already love it," she said quietly.

That stopped him.

Because she sounded exactly like her mother.

Mel leaned against the Falcon tiredly.

"When did this happen?"

Diane looked toward the strip.

"Probably years ago."

Rainwater dripped somewhere nearby.

Finally Mel sighed deeply.

"You know what scares me most?"

Diane shook her head.

Mel looked at her honestly.

"You're not doing this to impress boys."

She blinked.

"What?"

"If this was about boys, I could stop it."

Long silence.

Then:
"You actually want it."

Diane looked down at her hands on the steering wheel.

Then nodded once.

That was the moment Mel understood he'd already lost the argument.

Because wanting mattered.

Real racers always wanted it for reasons deeper than attention.

Mel rubbed his face exhausted.

Then:
"You keep this slow."
"You keep this smart."
"And if I ever catch you on Route 9, I will personally bury you under this diner."

Diane stared.

"…that's it?"

Mel frowned.

"What, you expected a speech?"

"A little."

Mel thought for a second.

Then:
"Your mother's gonna kill me."

That finally made Diane laugh.

And a few seconds later…

Mel did too.
