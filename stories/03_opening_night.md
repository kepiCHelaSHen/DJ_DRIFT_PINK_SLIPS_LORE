# Opening Night

April 15, 1957.

Opening night at Winterberry Acres Dragway was supposed to be about the future.

The county commissioner gave a speech nobody listened to.
Gene Hartwell cut the ribbon with oversized ceremonial scissors borrowed from the Chamber of Commerce.
Angie Ferraro sold hot dogs so fast she ran out of buns before sunset.
Kids climbed the bleachers.
Engines echoed across the valley.
For the first time in Potter County history, the racers had a place that was theirs.

No more backroads.
No more Route 9.
No more running from deputies through orchards at midnight.

A real strip.
A real Christmas tree.
Real timing equipment.
Real crowds.

Carmine Ferraro stood near the starting line that evening with his hands in his jacket pockets watching the sun drop behind the hills, and for the first time since the Route 9 accident, people said he looked happy.

Then she showed up.

Nobody in Winterberry Acres had seen the girl before.

She arrived alone just before dusk in a pale blue '56 Monarch Continental convertible with Riverside plates and enough chrome to blind a man at fifty yards.

The entire strip noticed.

Girls like her didn't come to Winterberry Acres.

Movie-star pretty.
White scarf tied around her hair.
Cat-eye sunglasses even though the sun was almost gone.
Red lipstick.
Long white gloves.
The kind of woman who looked expensive just standing still.

She parked near the fence and leaned against the Continental like she belonged there.

Every man under forty completely forgot whatever conversation he was having.

DJ Moretti nearly walked into a trash can staring at her.

Smooth Ray Cavanaugh adjusted his hair three separate times before walking over.

Got rejected in under thirty seconds.

According to Ray:
"She looked at me like I was a parking ticket."

That only made things worse.

By eight o'clock half the strip was trying to figure out who she was.

Some thought she was from Hollywood.
Some thought she was somebody's rich daughter from Riverside.
Nicky Ferris swore he'd seen her at a casino near Bakersfield.
Wes Buckley claimed her car had racing tires hidden under the stock hubcaps.

Then Henderson Cole noticed her.

That mattered.

Because Henderson didn't notice anybody.

He walked over after his second win of the night, still smelling like burnt fuel and hot oil, wiping his hands with a rag.

People literally stopped conversations to watch.

The king of the strip meeting the mystery girl.

She looked Henderson up and down once and said:
"You drive better than you dress."

Nobody had ever spoken to Henderson Cole that way.

Not and survived socially.

Henderson smirked.

Just a little.

That was enough for the gossip to spread through the crowd like wildfire.

By nine-thirty the entire strip had decided they were either:

secretly in love
secretly fighting
secretly married
or secretly dangerous

Nobody knew which.

Then came the challenge.

The girl — whose name she finally admitted was Vivian Laurent — pointed at Henderson's black Drifter and asked:
"That really the fastest car in this county?"

Henderson said:
"So far."

Vivian smiled.

"Cute answer."

Then she pointed toward the far end of the pits.

Toward a car nobody had paid attention to all night.

A dark green Falcon Starliner sitting half-hidden behind an Atlas hauler.

Quiet.
Plain.
No chrome tricks.
No crowd around it.

"Race my driver," she said.

Now everybody was paying attention.

The driver stepped out.

Tall.
Lean.
Older than Henderson by maybe ten years.
Wore leather driving gloves and a cream-colored jacket despite the heat.
Never said a word.

Just looked at Henderson like he already knew the outcome.

Carmine immediately hated the situation.

You could feel it.

Too much money.
Too much ego.
Too many people.
Opening night.
Brand-new track.
All the old fears sitting just beneath the surface.

But the crowd wanted it.

Badly.

So the race happened.

Winner takes the gate money from the entire night.

Biggest pot Winterberry Acres had ever seen.

The two cars rolled to the line just after ten.

The whole strip went dead quiet.

Even the announcer stopped talking.

Henderson in the black Drifter.
The stranger in the green Starliner.

Tree dropped.

The launch shook the valley.

Henderson got the holeshot.
Barely.

Half track —
dead even.

Big end —
still dead even.

People were screaming.

Then, right before the finish —

the Starliner twitched.

Just once.

Small movement.
Most people barely noticed it.

But Henderson did.

The green car drifted toward the wall for half a second before correcting.

Too late.

Henderson crossed first by less than a hood length.

The crowd exploded.

Opening night.
New track.
New king defending the crown.

Perfect story.

Except when the stranger climbed out of the Starliner afterward, his hands were shaking.

Badly.

Vivian saw it immediately.

So did Carmine.

The stranger lit a cigarette and couldn't even get the lighter steady.

Then Carmine noticed something else.

Blood.

Just a little.

Running from beneath the man's glove.

Heart attack, maybe.
Nerve damage.
War injury.
Nobody knew.

The stranger handed Vivian the keys without saying a word.

Then he walked off into the dark beyond the pit lights.

And nobody in Winterberry Acres ever saw him again.

Not once.

Vivian stayed another twenty minutes.

Long enough to walk over to Henderson one final time.

She looked at him quietly for a second and said:
"You know he almost had you."

Henderson replied:
"Almost don't count."

Vivian smiled sadly.

"Sometimes it's all people get."

Then she got into the Continental and disappeared west down Route 9.

Gone.

By midnight she had already become a legend.

People argued about her for years afterward.

Who she was.
Who the driver was.
Why his hands shook.
Why Vivian looked sad when Henderson won.

The old-timers still argue about it at Mel's.

But Carmine always says the same thing whenever the story comes up:

"Opening night taught me something important."

"What's that?" people ask.

And Carmine stares out toward the strip lights before answering:

"Fastest car and fastest man ain't always the same thing."
