## Tier 1 — Boss Characters

### #1. Eddie Malone — "Slick Eddie"
**DOB:** March 15, 1935 | **Age:** 22 | **Kenmore High '53** (barely graduated)

**Family:** Mother Dorothy Malone works as a seamstress on Oak Street. Father Frank Malone was a mechanic at Garvey Falcon who drank himself out of the job and left the family when Eddie was 14. Eddie has a younger sister, Marie (16), who is embarrassed by him. The Malones live in a rented house on Seventh Street, south of the railroad tracks.

**Romantic:** On-again, off-again with Patsy Caldwell (#87). They fight constantly — usually about Eddie prioritizing the car over her. When they're off, Eddie flirts with anything that moves but never follows through. He's more attached to Patsy than he admits. Had a brief thing with a girl from Millbury that Patsy doesn't know about.

**Friendships:** Runs with Danny Finch (#18) and Ronnie Dubowski (#41). Danny is his loyal sidekick, Ronnie is the hanger-on who talks too much. Eddie considers himself the leader but Danny is the one people actually like.

**Feuds:** Disrespects Ruby Red (#2) constantly — calls her lucky, says girls can't race, will regret all of it. Has a simmering rivalry with Henderson Cole (#9) but won't challenge him because he knows he'll lose. Talks trash about Theo Rice (#4) behind his back — calls him "college boy" — but is secretly intimidated by Theo's precision.

**Backstory:** Eddie rebuilt his '57 Starliner from a wreck he bought at Watkins Salvage for $200 when he was 21, in late 1956. Clyde Watkins (#52) gave him the car at a discount because he felt bad for the kid. Eddie spent two years in Iron Jack's garage learning to wrench, though he'll never admit Jack taught him everything. He works part-time at Big Al's (#69) flipping burgers, which he considers beneath him. His dream is to be the fastest racer in Potter County. His nightmare is ending up like his father.

### #2. Ruby Delgado — "Ruby Red"
**DOB:** August 22, 1938 | **Age:** 19 | **Kenmore High '56** (honor roll)

**Family:** Father Miguel Delgado ran Delgado Auto Repair, the only shop in the county that hired women. The shop went under in '55 when Miguel got sick — stomach cancer, died in March '56. Ruby's mother Carmen works at the Piggly Wiggly. Ruby has no siblings. She lives with her mother in a small house on Pine Street. The Stingray was her father's unfinished project — a rolling chassis with no motor when he died. Ruby finished it herself using skills he taught her.

**Romantic:** Currently single and not looking. Briefly dated Sam Otero (#54) in high school — they're still close friends but there was no spark. Every boy at the strip has tried. She turned down Smooth Ray (#23) so coldly he didn't speak to her for a month. There are rumors she and Tommy Pierce (#7) have feelings for each other but neither has acted on it.

**Friendships:** Sam Otero (#54) is her only real friend. Iron Jack (#3) is the closest thing she has to a mentor — he's the only racer who treated her as an equal from day one. She has a quiet mutual respect with Lindsay Smith (#10) — the only other woman racing regularly. They don't hang out but they nod at each other on the starting line.

**Feuds:** Eddie Malone (#1) dismisses her constantly. She doesn't engage — she just beats him and says nothing, which makes it worse. Del Hartley (#35) once told her to "go home and bake a cake." She beat him by three car lengths the next Friday and parked next to him in the pits without a word. Del hasn't spoken to her since.

**Backstory:** Ruby learned to wrench in her father's shop starting at age 10. By 14 she could rebuild a carburetor blindfolded. When her father died, the shop's equipment was sold to pay medical bills, but Ruby kept the Stingray and every tool her father owned. She finished the car in six months, working alone in the garage behind her house. The first time she staged it at the strip, the flagman laughed. She ran a twelve-eight. Nobody laughed after that. She doesn't race for money or trophies. She races because it's the last thing she and her father shared, and every pass keeps him alive.

### #3. Jack Drummond — "Iron Jack"
**DOB:** January 8, 1910 | **Age:** 47 | **Did not attend Kenmore High** (grew up in Tennessee, moved west in 1945)

**Family:** Never married. Came close once — a woman named Helen who left because she couldn't compete with the cars. Jack doesn't talk about her. No children. His shop is called "Drummond & Sons" because he liked how it sounded — the "sons" are every young racer he's ever mentored. Lives in a small apartment above the garage on Third Street.

**Romantic:** Widower to the road. Helen left in 1950. He's been alone since. Mel's wife occasionally tries to set him up with women from church. He always finds an excuse. Rita Vance (#72) at the diner has a crush on him that everyone can see except Jack.

**Friendships:** Gene Hartwell (#13) is his oldest friend — they served together in the Pacific and came home to build cars. Abe Prescott (#99) is the third member of their generation. Wes Buckley (#31) is a fellow veteran and drinking buddy (coffee, not liquor — Jack quit drinking in '48). Jack mentors Jimmy Dawes (#8), who he sees as the son he never had.

**Feuds:** None active. Jack is too old and too respected for feuds. He has a disapproving eye on Eddie Malone (#1) — sees his own reckless youth reflected and worries Eddie will wreck. Had a falling out with Henderson Cole (#9) in '54 over Henderson's unsportsmanlike conduct (Henderson refused to shake hands after a loss). They're civil now but not warm.

**Backstory:** Born in the Tennessee hills. Ran moonshine for his uncle starting at 15 — learned to drive fast on dirt roads at night with the headlights off. Enlisted in '42, served in the Pacific. Came west in '45 with Gene Hartwell and four hundred dollars of mustering-out pay. Worked at garages around the valley until he saved enough to open his own in '49. His '49 Comet Rocket 88 is the car he bought new — the only new car he ever owned. It has 90,000 miles and every one of them is a story. He'll race it until the day he dies, and he has told Wes Buckley that when that day comes, the Rocket 88 gets buried with him.

## Tier 2 — Elite Racers

### #4. Theo Rice — "The Professor"
**DOB:** September 3, 1929 | **Age:** 28 | **Did not attend Kenmore High** (moved to Winterberry Acres in 1954 after college)

**Family:** Father Walter Rice is a retired schoolteacher. Mother passed in '52. Older brother Harvey Rice (#45) co-owns Valley Performance. Younger cousin Jimmy Rice (#74) just started racing — Theo is teaching him the analytical approach. The Rice family lives on Elm Avenue — respectable side of town.

**Romantic:** Married to Margaret Rice (nee Thornton) since 1953. Margaret is a librarian at the county library. She tolerates the racing because Theo approaches it scientifically, not recklessly. They have one daughter, Susan, age 2. Margaret is pregnant with their second.

**Friendships:** Close with his brother Harv (#45) — they complement each other perfectly (Theo is the brain, Harv is the mouth). Respects Iron Jack (#3) for his experience. Has a working relationship with Doc Henderson (#73) — they swap diagnostic techniques (engines and patients). Friendly with Wes Buckley (#31) who respects anyone who treats a car like a science.

**Feuds:** Eddie Malone (#1) calls him "college boy" and mocks his notebook approach. Theo doesn't engage — he just runs faster times. Henderson Cole (#9) doesn't like that Theo is closing the gap analytically while Henderson relies on instinct. It's a philosophical rivalry as much as a racing one.

**Backstory:** Engineering degree from Cal Poly. Worked at the automotive division in Detroit for two years before moving to Winterberry Acres to be closer to his aging father. Now works as an engineer at the county water department — boring job, brilliant mind. He bought the '53 Sterling Starliner because it was the most technologically advanced car available. He tunes it with instruments, not intuition — air-fuel gauges, pyrometers, a notebook full of data. Every pass is logged, analyzed, and optimized. The other racers think he's taking the fun out of it. His times say otherwise.

### #5. DJ Moretti — "DJ Drift"
**DOB:** June 12, 1932 | **Age:** 25 | **Kenmore High '50**

**Family:** Father Dominic Moretti Sr. built DJ's Drive-In in 1948 and ran it until a heart attack in '55. DJ took over at 23. Mother Lucia still makes the popcorn on weekends. DJ has two younger sisters — Gina (22, married, lives in Riverside) and Sofia (18, works the ticket booth at the drive-in). The Morettis live in a house on Vine Street behind the pool hall.

**Romantic:** Currently dating Shirley Brennan, a nurse at County General. They've been together six months. Before Shirley, DJ dated half the girls in town — he's charming and everybody knows him. Briefly dated Lindsay Smith (#10) in '55 — ended when they realized they were better as friends. Still slightly awkward about it.

**Friendships:** DJ knows everyone. Literally everyone. He's the social hub — the drive-in is where all the car guys gather, and DJ is the host. Close friends with Tommy Pierce (#7) and Skylar Johnson (#6) — they grew up together. Carmine Ferraro (#11) trusts DJ to MC events at the strip. DJ is the unofficial announcer.

**Feuds:** None — DJ is too likeable for feuds. He has a mild tension with Nicky Ferris (#29) because Nicky runs gambling operations near the drive-in and DJ doesn't want that heat associated with his business. They stay civil but DJ won't let Nicky set up on his property.

**Backstory:** DJ got his nickname because he started as a record spinner at high school dances. "DJ" just stuck — nobody calls him Dominic. He inherited the drive-in and turned it into the center of youth culture in Winterberry Acres. By day he runs the business — scheduling movies, ordering supplies, managing the carhops. By night he races a built '55 Falcon Bel-Star that he and Tommy Pierce (#7) built together in Drummond's garage. He's fast enough to be elite but doesn't take it as seriously as the top guys because the drive-in is his real passion. He wants to open a chain of drive-ins across the country. The racing is the hobby. The business is the dream.

### #6. Skylar Johnson — "Sky"
**DOB:** February 28, 1937 | **Age:** 20 | **Kenmore High '55**

**Family:** Father Raymond Johnson is a bank manager at First National on First Street. Mother Evelyn is active in the garden club and church — horrified by Skylar's racing. Older brother Marcus Johnson (#55) works at the lumber yard and also races, though he's quieter about it. Younger brother Dwight (15) worships Skylar. Distant cousin Ralph Johnson (#84) lives across town. The Johnsons live on Elm Avenue — one of the nicer houses in town.

**Romantic:** Currently seeing Carol Ann Frazier, a Kenmore High senior. It's casual — Carol Ann doesn't know about the racing and Skylar hasn't told her. Previously dated Bonnie Carmichael (#90) briefly when she first moved to town, but she was more interested in engines than in him. He was baffled.

**Friendships:** Best friends with Tommy Pierce (#7) and DJ Moretti (#5) — the three of them are inseparable. They graduated within a year of each other and have been a crew since sophomore year. Also close with Jimmy Dawes (#8), who he sees as a younger brother figure. Gets along with everybody at the strip because his rich-kid background means he buys rounds at Mel's.

**Feuds:** His father Raymond disapproves violently of the racing — this is Skylar's main conflict. Raymond threatened to cut him off financially, so Skylar got a job at the hardware store to prove he could support himself. Eddie Malone (#1) resents Skylar's privilege — "must be nice to race with daddy's money" — even though Skylar bought his Thunderhawk with his own savings.

**Backstory:** Skylar had every advantage — good family, good house, good school. He was supposed to go to college and become a banker like his father. Instead he fell in love with cars after Tommy Pierce gave him a ride in his Falcon junior year. Skylar bought a '57 Sterling Thunderhawk with money from his summer job and started racing without telling his parents. His mother found out when Lorraine Prescott mentioned seeing him at the strip. There was a family crisis. Skylar held firm — racing is who he is, not what his parents planned for him. He's fearless on the strip, sometimes too fearless. Iron Jack has warned him twice about pushing past his skill level.

### #7. Tommy Pierce — "Tommy Two-Time"
**DOB:** November 5, 1936 | **Age:** 21 | **Kenmore High '54**

**Family:** Father Arthur Pierce works at the plant. Mother Betty is a homemaker. Tommy has a younger sister, Linda (17), who is dating a boy her parents don't approve of (the boy drives a hot rod — sound familiar). The Pierces live on Maple Street in a tidy three-bedroom. Arthur knows Tommy races but has made his peace with it as long as Tommy keeps his job and stays out of jail.

**Romantic:** Has quiet, unspoken feelings for Ruby Delgado (#2). They talk at the strip, he brings her coffee, she lets him look at her engine — which is more intimacy than she shows anyone. He hasn't made a move because he respects her space and is terrified of rejection. Previously dated a girl named Janet who moved to Riverside. Currently "between girlfriends" according to DJ, which means pining for Ruby.

**Friendships:** Best friends with DJ Moretti (#5) and Skylar Johnson (#6). Built his car with DJ in Iron Jack's garage. Tommy is the glue of the group — DJ is the social one, Skylar is the bold one, Tommy is the steady one. Also friends with Ruby (#2), the only person he's nervous around. Iron Jack (#3) thinks highly of Tommy's consistency.

**Feuds:** Got the nickname "Two-Time" because he always asks for a rematch when he loses. It's not a negative — people find it endearing. He has a minor rivalry with Bobby Strand (#14) because they're close in speed and always end up in the same bracket. No hard feelings, just competitive.

**Backstory:** Tommy is the everyman. Good kid, good family, works hard, races clean. He's not the fastest and he's not the flashiest but he's the most consistent mid-twelve racer at the strip. He built his red '55 Falcon Bel-Star from stock over two years in Iron Jack's garage, paying Jack in labor (sweeping the shop, running errands) since he couldn't afford shop rates. Jack taught him everything and Tommy absorbed it like a sponge. He works at the phone company as a lineman — steady work, good pay, union benefits. His mother approves. His dream is to own a speed shop someday, which is why he's been shadowing Harv Rice (#45) at Valley Performance on weekends.

### #8. Jimmy Dawes — "Jimmy the Kid"
**DOB:** April 20, 1939 | **Age:** 18 | **Kenmore High '57** (just graduated in June)

**Family:** Father Howard Dawes died in Korea in 1951. Mother Elaine works at the Piggly Wiggly — same shift as Ruby's mother Carmen. They're friends, which is how Jimmy first met Ruby at a grocery store picnic. Jimmy has a younger brother, Robert (14), who follows Jimmy everywhere. The Dawes family lives on Seventh Street, not far from the Malones. Elaine Dawes worries about Jimmy racing but trusts Iron Jack's guidance.

**Romantic:** Too focused on racing to date seriously. Has a crush on Sofia Moretti (#5 DJ's sister, works at the drive-in). She's aware and thinks it's cute but hasn't encouraged it. Jesse Yamamoto (#82) tried to set Jimmy up with a girl from church. It went badly — Jimmy talked about camshafts for forty-five minutes.

**Friendships:** Iron Jack (#3) is his mentor, father figure, and the most important person in his life. Jimmy spends more time at Drummond & Sons Garage than at home. Jack trusts Jimmy with customer cars, which is the highest compliment Jack gives. Also close with Tommy Pierce (#7) and the Tier 2 crew. Eddie Malone (#1) tries to recruit Jimmy to his crew — Jimmy politely refuses because he knows Jack wouldn't approve.

**Feuds:** None yet — he's too new. But his natural talent makes older, slower racers nervous. Bobby Strand (#14) got beaten by Jimmy in eliminations and hasn't gotten over it. There's a building tension between Jimmy and Eddie (#1) because they're the same age group but on opposite paths — Eddie is reckless instinct, Jimmy is disciplined skill.

**Backstory:** Jimmy grew up without a father and found one in Iron Jack. He started hanging around the garage at 14, sweeping floors, organizing tools. Jack saw something in him — patience, good hands, a quiet focus. By 16, Jimmy was doing valve adjustments. By 17, he was rebuilding carburetors. Jack gave him the '53 Falcon Bel-Star as a graduation present — a car Jack had taken in trade from a customer who couldn't pay. Jimmy built the motor himself under Jack's supervision. His first pass at the strip was a thirteen-one. His most recent was a twelve-four. He's improving faster than anyone has ever seen. Gene Hartwell (#13) says Jimmy reminds him of a young Jack Drummond, which is the highest praise anyone has ever given.

### #9. Henderson Cole — "Henderson"
**DOB:** July 19, 1922 | **Age:** 35 | **Did not attend Kenmore High** (moved to Winterberry Acres from San Bernardino in 1953)

**Family:** Divorced. Ex-wife Marilyn lives in San Bernardino with their two kids (son Dale, 10; daughter Karen, 8). Henderson sends money every month but rarely visits. Lives alone in a rented house on the edge of town near Route 9. No family in Winterberry Acres — he's an outsider who became the king.

**Romantic:** Single since the divorce in '52. Dates occasionally but nothing sticks — he's married to the Drifter. A waitress at the Starlight Room named Joan has been trying to get his attention for months. He's oblivious.

**Friendships:** Few close friends. Henderson is respected but not well-liked — he's too proud, too intense. Del Hartley (#35) is the closest thing he has to a friend, and even that's competitive. Carmine Ferraro (#11) and Henderson have a working relationship — Henderson is the draw that fills the bleachers. Gene Hartwell (#13) remembers when Henderson first showed up and thinks he's good for the sport but bad at being human.

**Feuds:** His rivalry with the Riverside Kid (Darnell Price, #100) is the defining conflict of the current season. Henderson also has unresolved tension with Iron Jack (#3) from a handshake refusal in '54. Pete Santos (#17) has been chasing a rematch for two years. Henderson doesn't hate anybody — he just doesn't notice them unless they're fast enough to matter.

**Backstory:** Henderson was a professional-level racer in San Bernardino before his marriage fell apart. Marilyn said it was the racing or the family. He chose the racing. Moved to Winterberry Acres because it was cheaper and the competition was softer — he could be the biggest fish in a smaller pond. His black '51 Comet Drifter with the stroked flathead and Hilborn injection is the fastest car that regularly races at the Acres. He's won fourteen races in a row. The Fourth of July grudge match against the Riverside kid was the first time anyone seriously threatened his reign. He won 2-1 but it rattled him. He knows the gap is closing and he's spending every night in his garage trying to find more speed.

### #10. Lindsay Smith — "Red Line Lindsay"
**DOB:** October 10, 1935 | **Age:** 22 | **Kenmore High '53**

**Family:** Father Douglas Smith is a foreman at the plant. Mother Barbara is a homemaker. Younger brother Charlie Smith (#75, "Smitty") just started racing — Lindsay taught him. The Smiths live on Oak Street. Douglas was initially furious about Lindsay racing but came around after watching her win three consecutive bracket rounds. Barbara still prays every Friday night.

**Romantic:** Single by choice. Dated DJ Moretti (#5) in 1955 — they were good together but better as friends. The breakup was mutual and clean. Bobby Strand (#14) has been asking her out for a year. She keeps saying no because Bobby is a mess with money and she doesn't need that in her life. There's a quiet attraction to Theo Rice (#4) that will never go anywhere because he's happily married.

**Friendships:** Works at Valley Auto Supply under Mickey Haines (#59), which means she knows every part number and every racer's build. This makes her valuable and informed. Has a quiet solidarity with Ruby Red (#2) — they don't socialize but they respect each other deeply. Lindsay is more outgoing than Ruby and handles the boys' club atmosphere with humor rather than silence. Close with Loretta Simms (#83), who she encouraged to start racing.

**Feuds:** Some of the old guard resent a woman working at the speed shop and racing. Norm Bassett (#63) once told her "the parts counter is for men." She memorized his entire engine spec from his purchase history and recited it back to him, then suggested three upgrades he'd missed. He hasn't spoken to her since but he implemented all three. Del Hartley (#35) won't race her because "I don't race girls." She's waiting for him to run out of excuses.

**Backstory:** Lindsay grew up in a house full of car people — her father raced stock cars after the war, her uncle had a speed shop in Riverside. She grew up surrounded by tools and engines. When she told her father she wanted to race, he said "then you better be faster than the boys because they won't give you anything." She is. Her white '56 Falcon Stingray is the second-fastest car at the strip behind Henderson's Drifter. She revs to the redline on every pass — hence the nickname — and she has the reaction time to back it up. She wants to open her own speed shop someday. Working at Valley Auto is the apprenticeship.

### #11. Carmine Ferraro — "Carmine"
**DOB:** December 1, 1919 | **Age:** 38 | **Did not attend Kenmore High** (grew up in Los Angeles, moved to Winterberry Acres in 1948)

**Family:** Unmarried. Lives alone on Vine Street. His cousin Angie Ferraro (#71) is his closest family in town — she runs the hot dog cart at the strip. Her brother Frankie (#89) works with her. Carmine's parents are in L.A. — his father Salvatore runs a produce stand in the Italian neighborhood. Carmine visits at Christmas.

**Romantic:** Has never had a serious relationship since moving to Winterberry Acres. He pours all of his emotional energy into the strip. Angie has tried to set him up with women from church. He goes on one date, talks about timing systems for an hour, and never gets a second call.

**Friendships:** Respected by everyone but close to few. Gene Hartwell (#13) is the one person who understands what the strip means to Carmine. Wes Buckley (#31) is a quiet friend — they drink coffee together at Mel's every morning at 6 AM before anyone else is awake. Carmine trusts DJ Moretti (#5) with the PA system and event coordination. Henderson Cole (#9) is a business asset more than a friend.

**Feuds:** Eunice Whitfield (non-racer NPC) is his nemesis. Her petitions and complaints are a constant threat to the strip. Carmine handles her with diplomacy but privately finds her exhausting. Nicky Ferris (#29) operates gambling at the strip, which Carmine tolerates because he can't stop it — but he doesn't like it.

**Backstory:** Carmine raced a candy apple red '50 Comet Drifter in the early fifties. He was fast and fearless — one of the best on the old runway. In 1951, he was racing a kid from Bakersfield on Route 9. The kid lost control on the curve past the Miller farm, went off the road, hit a tree. Broken back, broken legs, lost an eye. The kid — his name was Vincent Moreno — died in 1957 from complications. Carmine quit racing that night. He sold the Drifter and swore he'd never sit behind the wheel of a racing car again. Instead, he channeled everything into making racing safe. He organized the runway races, introduced timing and rules, and spearheaded the campaign for a sanctioned strip. The purple Comet Fury in his garage is the car he built to replace the Drifter. He's never raced it. Everyone wonders how fast it is. Carmine knows exactly how fast it is because he tested it alone at 3 AM one night. He will never tell anyone the time.

### #12. Virgil Stanton — "Virgil the Viper"

**Family:** Married to Dorothy Stanton. Two kids — Virgil Jr. (8) and Daisy (5). Lives on Orchard Drive in one of the newer houses. Dorothy tolerates the racing because Virgil is otherwise a model husband and father. She drew the line at three hundred and fifty dollars for the paint job. He bought her flowers the next day.

**Friendships:** Regular at Mel's Diner — sits in the back booth with the racers. Drinking buddies with Hank Calloway (#19) and Pete Santos (#17). Dempsey Hall (#64) painted his car and they've been friends since. Virgil is the guy who brings donuts to the Saturday work days at the strip.

**Feuds:** Mild rivalry with Ray Cavanaugh (#23) because Ray hit on Dorothy at a car show once. Virgil didn't fight him — he just beat him by four car lengths the next Friday and parked his beautiful candy apple red Falcon right next to Ray's scruffy Drifter. Message received.

**Backstory:** Navy veteran, Pacific theater. Came home, married his girl, got a job at the county assessor's office. Boring work, exciting weekends. His car — the candy apple red '55 Falcon Bel-Star he calls "Ruby Queen" — is his masterpiece. Dempsey (#64) did the paint: red with pearl white scallops, blue-tipped flames on the hood, and "Ruby Queen" in white script on the trunk. It's the most beautiful car at the strip and Virgil knows it. He's competitive but fair, flashy but sincere. The kind of guy who shakes your hand whether he wins or loses and means it both times.

### #13. Gene Hartwell — "Old Gene"
**DOB:** October 22, 1899 | **Age:** 58 | **Did not attend Kenmore High** (grew up in Ohio, moved west in 1945)

**Family:** Married to Mabel Hartwell for 35 years. Three grown children — all moved away. Gene and Mabel live on Elm Avenue in a house they bought in '46. Mabel leaves the strip early every Friday because she's "heard enough engines for one lifetime." She secretly loves that Gene has something that keeps him young.

**Friendships:** Iron Jack (#3) is his oldest friend — they served together. Abe Prescott (#99) is the third member of their generation. These three men are the foundation of the racing community. Carmine Ferraro (#11) is like a son to Gene — Gene mentored Carmine when he first arrived in town. Gene is club president of the Valley Street Rod Association and everyone's grandfather figure.

**Feuds:** None. Gene is above feuds. Even Eunice Whitfield respects him (grudgingly). If Gene says something, the community listens. He is the moral authority of the strip.

**Backstory:** Born in Ohio. Served in the Pacific with Jack Drummond. Came west in '45 with nothing and built a life around cars. His '34 Comet Deuce with a flathead V-8 is the oldest racing car at the strip. He built it in 1947 and has raced it every year since. He runs fourteens now — not fast by modern standards — but nobody would dare disrespect his time. Gene wrote the proposal for the sanctioned drag strip, fought for it at the county commission, and organized the volunteer crews that built it. Without Gene Hartwell, there is no Winterberry Acres Dragway. He knows this and never mentions it.

### #14. Bobby Strand — "Bobby Brakes"
**DOB:** January 30, 1933 | **Age:** 24 | **Kenmore High '51**

**Family:** Single mother raised him — Dolores Strand works at the phone company. Father left when Bobby was 3. No siblings. Lives in a rented room on Seventh Street. His girlfriend Nancy Wheeler (#97) is learning to drive his car.

**Romantic:** Dating Nancy Wheeler (#97). She's patient with him but frustrated that he spends money on racing instead of saving for their future. He's asked Lindsay Smith (#10) out repeatedly — she always says no. Nancy doesn't know about the Lindsay attempts.

**Feuds:** Owes money to Nicky Ferris (#29) regularly. Betting problem. His nickname "Bobby Brakes" comes from the fact that his car's brakes are always the last thing he fixes — he puts speed before safety. Tommy Pierce (#7) is his bracket rival — they end up racing each other nearly every week.

**Backstory:** Bobby is the cautionary tale of talent without discipline. He's fast — genuinely fast — but he spends every dollar on parts and bets instead of rent and savings. His '56 Comet Fury runs low thirteens but the brakes squeal, the tires are bald, and the seat belt is held on with a wire hanger. Jack Drummond (#3) has refused to let Bobby race without fixing the belt. Bobby fixed the belt. Nothing else.

### #16. Frank Morelli — "Frankie Flat-Out"
**DOB:** July 7, 1930 | **Age:** 27 | **Kenmore High '48**
