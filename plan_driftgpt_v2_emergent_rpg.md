# DriftGPT v2 — Emergent RPG Dialogue System

## Vision

DJ DRIFT'S PINK SLIPS is not a racing game with NPC flavor text. It's an emergent RPG where the social layer IS the game and racing is the combat system. Every NPC conversation is generated by a custom model that understands the game world, tracks relationships, remembers history, and produces unique dialogue that no two players will ever see identically.

No dialogue trees. No branching scripts. No API calls to a cloud model. A 691M parameter model ships with the game and runs on the player's hardware in under 200ms.

## Phase 2: Deepen the World (1-2 weeks)

### Goal
Make Winterberry Acres a world rich enough that 40 hours of gameplay feels lived-in. Every detail becomes training data.

### World-Building Targets

**Characters (expand from 113 to ~150)**
- Add non-racer townspeople: bartenders, shop owners, teachers, mechanics' wives, the radio DJ's listeners
- Deepen existing NPC backstories: childhood, family, secrets, fears, dreams
- Map every relationship: who's friends, who's feuding, who's sleeping together, who owes who money
- Define each NPC's knowledge radius: what do they know, what have they heard, what are they wrong about

**Locations (expand from 8 to ~15)**
- Add: pool hall, church, Elm Avenue houses, Route 9 overlook, dry lake beds, Dawson Motors used car lot, high school parking lot
- Each location needs: physical description, who's there when, what conversations happen there, what you can overhear
- Locations should have moods that shift: the diner at 2am vs noon, the strip before vs after a crash

**Timeline & Events**
- Week-by-week calendar of Winterberry Acres from January 1959 onward
- Key events: NHRA regional qualifier, Henderson vs Riverside Kid grudge match, Tony Marcone's loan shark operation exposed, the Route 9 accident memorial, Christmas at Mel's, spring formal at the high school
- These events create the world NPCs reference: "You hear about what happened at the strip last Friday?"

**Slang & Speech Patterns**
- Expand the 1950s slang dictionary per character: Eddie talks different from The Professor
- Regional American dialect markers
- Each NPC should have 3-5 signature phrases or verbal tics

**Economy & Stakes**
- How much does a car cost? What's a pink slip worth?
- Who has money, who's broke, who's in debt to Tony Marcone
- What does winning/losing a race actually cost someone emotionally and financially
- What does the player risk at each stage of reputation

**Secrets & Hidden Lore**
- Things only certain NPCs know (who is the Riverside Kid really?)
- Rumors that may or may not be true

**State Variable Spec**: See `docs/spec_state_variables.md` for the complete variable system (6 NPC→Player variables, 4 player state variables, 5 NPC internal state variables, 5 NPC→NPC relationship types, ~80-100 new special tokens).
- Information that spreads through the social network over time
- Easter eggs for players who dig deep into conversations

### Lore-to-Training Pipeline

Every piece of lore follows this pipeline:

```
Lore Document (world bible)
    ↓
Character-Specific Context Cards
    (what does Eddie know about this event?)
    ↓
Training Example Generator (Claude CLI)
    (generate 5-10 conversations where this lore surfaces naturally)
    ↓
Fine-Tune Dataset
```

**Key principle**: Lore that doesn't become training data is invisible to the model. For every world-building detail, ask: "What NPC would talk about this, and in what situation?"

### Output
- Expanded world bible (docs/lore/)
- 150+ NPC profiles with relationships mapped
- Event timeline
- Character knowledge matrices (who knows what)

## Phase 3: Re-Pretrain with 4096 Context (3-4 days)

### Goal
Rebuild the base model with a 4096-token context window so it can handle multi-turn conversations and world context.

### What Changes
| Parameter | v1 (current) | v2 |
|-----------|-------------|-----|
| `block_size` | 512 | 4096 |
| Everything else | Same | Same |

Architecture stays identical: 32 layers, 1152 hidden, 18 heads, 691M params. Only the positional embedding range changes (RoPE scales to 4096).

### Pre-Training Corpus
Same 500k-word 1950s dialogue corpus, but:
- Concatenate into longer sequences (4096 tokens each instead of 512)
- Add multi-paragraph dialogue scenes so the model learns to track context over longer spans
- Optionally add more source material: 1950s novels, screenplays, period-accurate dialogue

### Training Config
```
block_size: 4096
batch_size: 4 (A100 80GB can handle this in FP32)
grad_accum: 4
effective_batch: 16
steps: ~15k-20k
device: cuda (A100 or H100)
```

### Time & Cost
| GPU | Estimated Time | Cost |
|-----|---------------|------|
| A100 80GB | ~60-72 hours | ~$60-70 |
| H100 80GB | ~30-40 hours | ~$50-60 |

### Deliverable
`final_v2.pt` — Pre-trained 691M model with 4096 context window.

### Goal

```json
    },
    {
      "role": "user",
      "content": "Player slides into the booth across from Eddie."
    },
    {
      "role": "assistant",
      "content": "Hey — hey, sit down, yeah. You, uh, you want coffee or somethin'?"
    },
    {
      "role": "user",
      "content": "Player asks Eddie what he's heard about Marcone's bounty."
    },
    {
      "role": "assistant",
      "content": "Keep your voice down, will ya? Marcone's got ears everywhere. Look, all I know is he's offerin' a hundred bucks to anyone who takes your car in pinks. A hundred bucks. That's serious bread."
    },
    {
      "role": "user",
      "content": "Player asks who Eddie thinks would take that deal."
    },
    {
      "role": "assistant",
      "content": "Anybody broke enough. And in this town, that's half the strip. Watch your back at night, that's all I'm sayin'."
    }
  ]
}
```

### New Special Tokens
```
<|world_context|>    — begins world context block
<|end_context|>      — ends world context block
```

### Data Generation Strategy

Use Claude CLI as a data factory (per established workflow):

**Tier 1: Character-specific deep conversations (1,500 examples)**
- 15 conversations per NPC × 100 NPCs
- Each conversation: 4-8 exchanges
- Varying moods, relationship states, locations, times
- Include world context that the NPC would realistically know

**Tier 2: Cross-character conversations (1,500 examples)**
- Player talks to NPC A about NPC B
- Gossip, rumors, relationship dynamics
- "What do you think about Ruby?" "Did you hear about Henderson?"

**Tier 3: World event reactions (1,000 examples)**
- Player talks to various NPCs before/after major events
- "Henderson just lost" → every NPC reacts differently
- Pink slip wins, arrests, races, relationship changes

**Tier 4: Free-text conversations (1,000 examples)**
- Player uses direct speech instead of narrated actions
- Mix of casual chat, probing questions, insults, flirting
- Tests the model's ability to handle unexpected input

**Tier 5: Edge cases (500 examples)**
- NPC doesn't want to talk (angry, scared, busy)
- NPC lies or deflects
- NPC changes subject
- NPC references something the player doesn't know about yet

### Quality Control
- Each batch reviewed for: character voice consistency, period accuracy, state tag responsiveness
- Flag and regenerate examples where NPCs sound generic or break character
- Ensure world context is referenced naturally, not awkwardly shoehorned

### Output
~5,500 multi-turn conversations → ~25,000 training exchanges total.

## Phase 6: Build the Game Engine Memory System (1-2 weeks)

### Goal
Build the Godot-side systems that give the model "memory" by constructing rich context windows from game state.

### Component 1: Event Log

A persistent log of every significant game event.

```gdscript
# event_log.gd
var events: Array[Dictionary] = []

func log_event(event: Dictionary):
    # event = {
    #   "type": "race_result",
    #   "timestamp": game_time,
    #   "participants": ["player", "henderson"],
    #   "location": "strip",
    #   "outcome": "player_won_pinks",
    #   "details": "Player won Henderson's '51 Drifter in pink slips",
    #   "witnesses": ["eddie", "ruby", "tommy", "crowd"],
    #   "significance": 0.9  # how notable (0-1)
    # }
    events.append(event)
```

Event types:
- `race_result` — who raced, who won, stakes, witnesses
- `conversation` — who talked, key topics, sentiment shift
- `relationship_change` — significant respect/grudge/fear shifts
- `world_event` — things that happen independent of player (NPC-NPC races, arrests, gossip spread)
- `player_action` — bought a car, visited a new location, got arrested
- `npc_event` — Eddie got drunk, Ruby fixed her engine, Tony threatened someone

### Component 2: NPC Knowledge Graph

Each NPC has a knowledge radius — what they could realistically know.

```
Knowledge sources:
  - Direct witness (they were there)
  - Told by friend (one hop in social graph)
  - Town gossip (high-significance events, 2+ hops, with delay)
  - Doesn't know (low-significance, no connection)
```

Information decays and distorts as it passes through the social network:
- Eddie tells Tommy → Tommy knows accurately
- Tommy tells Mel → Mel knows approximately
- Mel tells a customer → it's now a rumor and might be wrong

This creates emergent situations where an NPC says something inaccurate because they heard it third-hand. The player can correct them or use the misinformation.

### Component 3: Context Builder

The critical piece. Selects relevant events and writes a natural-language summary for the prompt.

```gdscript
func build_context(npc_id: String, current_situation: Dictionary) -> String:
    var relevant_events = []

    # 1. Direct history with player (highest priority)
    relevant_events += get_events_involving(npc_id, "player", limit=5)

    # 2. Major events this NPC knows about
    relevant_events += get_known_events(npc_id, min_significance=0.7, limit=3)

    # 3. Recent events involving NPCs this character cares about
    var close_npcs = social_graph.get_close_npcs(npc_id)
    relevant_events += get_events_involving_any(close_npcs, limit=3)

    # 4. Sort by relevance (recency × significance × personal connection)
    relevant_events.sort_custom(relevance_score)

    # 5. Write natural language summary (template-based, ~100-200 tokens)
    return summarize_events(relevant_events, npc_id)
```

Example output for Eddie when the player is a legend:
```
Player took Henderson's '51 Drifter in pink slips on the Fourth of July.
Ruby rides with player now. Eddie lost to player twice but player showed
mercy both times. Tony Marcone has a bounty on player's car. Last Friday
Jimmy the Kid beat Carmine's quarter-mile record.
```

### Component 4: Conversation Manager

Manages the multi-turn conversation flow in-game.

```
[State Tags]           ~35 tokens
[World Context]        ~150 tokens (from context builder)
[Turn 1: user+npc]     ~80 tokens
[Turn 2: user+npc]     ~80 tokens
[Turn 3: user+npc]     ~80 tokens
[Current user input]   ~30 tokens
[NPC response space]   ~150 tokens
                       ─────────
Budget:                ~605 tokens of 4096 available
```

With 4096 tokens, you can fit ~6-8 exchange conversations with rich world context. Older turns get dropped (sliding window) if the conversation goes longer.

After each conversation ends:
- Log significant exchanges to the event log
- Update NPC relationship values
- Propagate notable information through the social graph (gossip spreads)

### Deliverable
Godot plugin: EventLog, KnowledgeGraph, ContextBuilder, ConversationManager.

## World-Building Depth Guide

### You Cannot Have Too Much Lore

For this system, lore depth directly equals dialogue quality. Here's what to flesh out:

**Layer 1: Public knowledge (everyone knows)**
- Race results, who drives what, who's dating who
- Business owners, town layout, upcoming events
- Slang, catchphrases, cultural references

**Layer 2: Social circle knowledge (friends know)**
- Personal problems, financial trouble, family drama
- Secret relationships, hidden ambitions, fears
- What happened at private gatherings

**Layer 3: Private knowledge (only close friends know)**
- Eddie's father left when he was 12
- Ruby cried when she finished her dad's Stingray
- Henderson's wife in San Bernardino doesn't know he races
- Jimmy the Kid has nightmares about Korea (his father's war)

**Layer 4: Secrets (maybe 1-2 people know)**
- Who is the Riverside Kid really?
- Tony Marcone's connection to organized crime in LA
- Why Carmine really quit racing (not just the accident)
- Reverend Comstock's gambling problem

**Layer 5: Things nobody knows yet**
- Events that will unfold as the game progresses
- Time bombs in the narrative (Tony's operation will be exposed)
- NPC arcs that play out independent of the player

### The Rule

For every lore detail, answer:
1. **Who knows this?** → Maps to the knowledge graph
2. **How would they talk about it?** → Becomes training data
3. **What would they get wrong about it?** → Gossip distortion
4. **When would it come up in conversation?** → Situational triggers
